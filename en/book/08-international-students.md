# 8. International Students: Language Schools, University Entrance, Graduate School Entrance, Professional Training Colleges

This section is about your money, your time and your personal freedom. It is written for people who hold Student status (留学): those who attend a language school first and then apply to a university or graduate school, those who go straight into a professional training college (専門学校), and those who are research students (研究生) at a university while waiting to sit the master's entrance exam. The three routes differ in their entry conditions, in how you are supervised along the way, and in the exits after graduation. If you pick the wrong school or miss one condition, what you lose is a year of tuition and time.

This section does not compare schools and does not teach you how to pass exams. It only writes the conditions fixed in laws and official documents. It uses the Landing Criteria Ministerial Ordinance (上陸基準省令) under the Immigration Control Act, the website of the Immigration Services Agency (出入国在留管理庁, "the ISA"), the website of the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (文部科学省, "MEXT"), the website of the Japan Student Services Organization (日本学生支援機構, "JASSO"), and the notice of the Chinese Service Center for Scholarly Exchange (教育部留学服务中心, CSCSE) under China's Ministry of Education. Everything is as of 2026-10-05.

Several items that every international student needs are in other sections and are not repeated here:

- Part-time work of 28 hours a week, 8 hours a day during long vacations, and the permission lapsing once you leave school: see Section 1, Item 7 (get permission for activities outside your status before taking part-time work).
- Part-time wages can be exempt from income tax, and the form must go through your employer: see Section 4, Item 10 (part-time wages of students from China can be exempt from income tax).
- At age 20 you must join the National Pension, and students can apply for the special payment system: see Section 3, Item 1 (if you live in Japan between ages 20 and 60, you must join the National Pension) and Section 3, Item 2 (students and people who cannot afford to pay: apply for the special system or an exemption).
- Moving, leaving school and transferring schools each need a notification within 14 days: see Section 1, Item 2 (register your new address within 14 days of moving) and Section 1, Item 3 (each time you leave a job, start a job or change organizations).
- If you have not been attending school for 3 consecutive months or more, your status of residence can be revoked: see Section 1, Item 4 (do not stay without a job for 3 months or more after leaving). That item applies to Student status in the same way.

### 1. For a school where you only study Japanese, confirm before paying that it is a designated or accredited institution; otherwise you cannot get Student status
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Check the list once before you pay tuition.
- In plain words: If you want to study only Japanese in Japan under Student status, the school must be a Japanese-language institution designated by public notice of the Minister of Justice, or the course for study in Japan at an accredited Japanese-language institution. At a school outside these two categories, you can pay the tuition and still not get Student status.
- Benefit: Item (vi) of the Student (留学) entry in the Landing Criteria Ministerial Ordinance provides that where the applicant, at a university, specialized training college (専修学校), miscellaneous school (各種学校) or the like, is in the case of 「専ら日本語教育を受けようとする場合」 ("intending to receive exclusively Japanese-language education"), the educational institution must be 「告示日本語教育機関又は認定日本語教育機関」 ("a designated Japanese-language institution or an accredited Japanese-language institution"). For an accredited Japanese-language institution, this is limited to a person who receives Japanese-language education in the 「留学のための課程」 ("course for study in Japan"). Item (v) of the same entry defines a designated Japanese-language institution (告示日本語教育機関) as an institution that provides Japanese-language education to foreign nationals and that the Minister of Justice has designated by public notice after hearing the opinion of the Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology. It defines an accredited Japanese-language institution (認定日本語教育機関) as an institution provided for in Article 3, paragraph (1) of the Act on Accreditation of Japanese-Language Institutions.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法第七条第一項第二号の基準を定める省令（平成二年法務省令第十六号）留学の項 第五号・第六号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/402M50000010016>
- Notes: This section did not fetch the pages where the two lists can be looked up, so it gives no URLs. Ask immigration and MEXT respectively, or have the school show you the basis of its designation or accreditation. This section did not verify whether all designated institutions will be converted to accredited institutions in the future, or up to which year the transition runs. For the enrollment limit and the specific attendance figures, see Item 12 (a language school allows at most 2 years). If you use an agent, see Section 21, Item 9 and the items on agents in Section 32 of the original book.

### 2. You must be able to explain where your living expenses come from; the school is obliged to keep records of your attendance, part-time work and studies
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: The living expenses of your study period themselves. Attend class on time in ordinary weeks.
- In plain words: One of the conditions for applying for Student status is having enough assets, scholarships or other means to pay your living expenses while you are in Japan. It is also acceptable if someone else pays them for you. The school that accepts you must have a management system that records its foreign students' attendance, whether they keep the part-time work rules, and how their studies are going.
- Benefit: Item (ii) of the Student entry in the Landing Criteria Ministerial Ordinance: 「申請人がその本邦に在留する期間中の生活に要する費用を支弁する十分な資産、奨学金その他の手段を有すること。ただし、申請人以外の者が申請人の生活費用を支弁する場合は、この限りでない。」 ("The applicant must have sufficient assets, scholarships or other means to pay the expenses required for living during the period of stay in Japan. However, this does not apply where a person other than the applicant pays the applicant's living expenses.") Item (ii)-2 requires of the educational institution the applicant is going to that 「当該教育機関において教育を受ける外国人の出席状況、法第十九条第一項の規定の遵守状況、学習の状況等を適正に管理する体制を整備していること」 ("it has in place a system to properly manage the attendance of foreign nationals receiving education at that institution, their compliance with Article 19, paragraph (1) of the Act, the state of their studies, and so on"). Article 19, paragraph (1) of the Act here is the restriction on activities outside your status.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法第七条第一項第二号の基準を定める省令 留学の項 第二号・第二号の二. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/402M50000010016>
- Notes: This is the standard at the time of entry. This section did not verify against the original text how immigration looks at attendance and grades when you extend your period of stay, or whether the school reports its records to immigration, so it writes no specific attendance-rate figure. The ordinance states no amount for how much money is "sufficient" or what proof you must submit. This section did not verify whether money earned from part-time work can count as a source of living expenses.

### 3. As a research student or auditing student at a university who mainly attends lectures, you must attend lectures for at least 10 hours a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. When you plan your timetable, make sure the lecture hours add up, and keep a record.
- In plain words: For a research student or auditing student who mainly attends lectures to get Student status, you must have been admitted through the school's admission selection, and you must attend lectures at that school for at least 10 hours a week.
- Benefit: Item (iii) of the Student entry in the Landing Criteria Ministerial Ordinance: where the applicant is in the case of 「専ら聴講による教育を受ける研究生又は聴講生として教育を受ける場合」 ("receiving education as a research student or auditing student who receives education exclusively by attending lectures"), the applicant must fall under item (i)(a) or (b), and 「当該教育を受ける教育機関が行う入学選考に基づいて入学の許可を受け、かつ、当該教育機関において一週間につき十時間以上聴講をすること」 ("be admitted on the basis of the admission selection conducted by the educational institution where the education is received, and attend lectures at that institution for at least ten hours per week").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法第七条第一項第二号の基準を定める省令 留学の項 第三号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/402M50000010016>
- Notes: This item is about research students (研究生) and auditing students (聴講生) who "receive education exclusively by attending lectures". The provision does not say separately whether a research student who does research in a laboratory and does not mainly attend lectures is included. This section draws no conclusion on that. Ask the staff in charge of international students at your school. A research student is not on a regular degree-conferring course. Whether it counts as the "进修" (advanced training) in the notice is discussed in the Notes to Item 11. How long you can stay as a research student is set by each university, and this section did not verify it.

### 4. To enter a professional training college you must first prove your Japanese is good enough: one full year of Japanese study, proof by examination, or one full year at a school in Japan, any one of the three
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: One year at a language school, or the registration fee and preparation time for one Japanese-language examination.
- In plain words: A foreign national who enters a specialized training college or miscellaneous school to study something other than Japanese must meet one of three conditions. One is at least one year of Japanese study in the course for study in Japan at a designated or accredited institution. One is proving by examination that your Japanese is good enough to take classes at that school. One is at least one year of study at a school in Japan such as an elementary school, junior high school, high school or university. The school must also have a full-time staff member whose job is life guidance for foreign students.
- Benefit: Item (v) of the Student entry in the Landing Criteria Ministerial Ordinance provides that where the applicant receives education at a specialized training college or miscellaneous school (except where the applicant receives exclusively Japanese-language education), the following must be met. (a) The applicant is 「一年以上の日本語教育を受けた者」 ("a person who has received Japanese-language education for one year or more") in the course for study in Japan at a designated Japanese-language institution or accredited Japanese-language institution, or 「専修学校若しくは各種学校において教育を受けるに足りる日本語能力を試験により証明された者」 ("a person whose Japanese-language ability sufficient to receive education at a specialized training college or miscellaneous school has been proven by examination"), or 「一年以上の教育を受けた者」 ("a person who has received education for one year or more") at a school provided for in Article 1 of the School Education Act (学校教育法), excluding kindergartens. (b) The educational institution has a full-time staff member in charge of life guidance for foreign students.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法第七条第一項第二号の基準を定める省令 留学の項 第五号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/402M50000010016>
- Notes: The ordinance does not say which examinations, or which level, count as "proven". That is in other immigration documents, which this section did not fetch, so it writes no level. This is the condition for getting the status of residence. Each professional training college can set its own entry conditions higher. Universities and graduate schools have no such item; their Japanese requirement is set by each school.

### 5. For university entrance, first check which EJU subjects your target school requires: there are only two sittings a year, in June and November, and there is no test site in mainland China
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: If you sit it in Japan, 12,000 yen for one subject and 23,000 yen for two or more subjects (fiscal 2026). Preparation time is counted in months.
- In plain words: The EJU (日本留学試験, Examination for Japanese University Admission for International Students) is held twice a year. In 2026 the dates are June 21 and November 8. The subjects are Japanese, Science, Japan and the World, and Mathematics. Which subjects you sit, and whether you answer in Japanese or English, is specified by the university you are applying to. You cannot choose both Science and Japan and the World. Registration closes more than three months before the test. The list of test sites outside Japan has no city in mainland China.
- Benefit: JASSO's implementation guidelines for fiscal 2026 state the following. The examination is for people who wish to enter a Japanese university or similar institution as a foreign student. The test dates are June 21, 2026 for the 1st session and November 8, 2026 for the 2nd session. The examinee chooses the subjects 「受験希望の大学等の指定に基づき」 ("based on the specification of the university or other institution the examinee wishes to apply to"). Japanese is 125 minutes: Reading comprehension and Listening and Listening-Reading comprehension are 0 to 400 points, and Writing is 0 to 50 points. Science is 80 minutes and 0 to 200 points, with 2 subjects chosen from Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Japan and the World (総合科目) is 80 minutes and 0 to 200 points. Mathematics is 80 minutes and 0 to 200 points, divided into Course 1 and Course 2. The remarks state 「理科と総合科目を同時に選択することはできない」 ("Science and Japan and the World cannot be chosen at the same time"). The examination languages are Japanese and English, declared at registration; the Japanese subject is set in Japanese only. The registration period in Japan is February 16 to March 12, 2026 at 17:00 for the 1st session, and July 6 to July 30 at 17:00 for the 2nd session. The examination fee in Japan is 12,000 yen for 1 subject and 23,000 yen for 2 or more subjects (tax included). The test locations listed outside Japan are cities in India, Indonesia, South Korea, Singapore, Sri Lanka, Thailand, Taiwan (Taipei), the Philippines, Vietnam, Hong Kong, Malaysia, Myanmar and Mongolia.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 日本学生支援機構. 2026年度 日本留学試験 実施要項. <https://www.jasso.go.jp/ryugaku/eju/about/eju_guide/2026.html>
- Notes: Dates and fees differ every fiscal year; these are for fiscal 2026. This section did not verify against the original text how many years a score stays usable, or whether you can pick your best sitting to submit. Follow each university's application guidelines (募集要項). Many universities also have their own examination, interview and English score requirements, and some do not use the EJU. For all of these, read the target school's guidelines for that year. If you are in mainland China, you either come to Japan to sit it, or go to a place on the list, or use an admission route that does not need the EJU.

### 6. Before applying to a university, confirm you have 12 years of schooling, and 16 years for a graduate school; if you fall short, there are other routes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Count your own years of schooling.
- In plain words: A person who has completed 12 years of school education in a foreign country is qualified for admission to a Japanese university. A person who has completed 16 years of school education in a foreign country is qualified for admission to a master's course. Doctoral courses in medicine, dentistry, pharmacy and veterinary medicine need 18 years. If you are short of years, on the university side you can first take a designated preparatory education course. On the graduate school side, the graduate school can carry out an individual admission qualification examination and recognize a person aged 22 or older.
- Benefit: MEXT's page on university admission qualifications lists 「外国において、学校教育における12年の課程を修了した者」 ("a person who has completed a 12-year course of school education in a foreign country"). For a course of less than 12 years, the person must also complete a designated preparatory education course or the like. The page notes that this means completing the course of the 12th year of regular school education in the foreign country. The page on admission qualifications for master's courses lists a person who has completed a 16-year course of school education in a foreign country (18 years for doctoral courses in medicine, dentistry, pharmacy or veterinary medicine), and also 「大学院において個別の入学資格審査により認めた22歳以上の者（施行規則第155条第1項第8号）」 ("a person aged 22 or older whom the graduate school has recognized through an individual admission qualification examination (Enforcement Regulation, Article 155, paragraph (1), item (viii))").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 文部科学省. 大学入学資格について. <https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/koutou/shikaku/07111314.htm>；文部科学省. 修士課程・博士課程（前期）の入学資格について. <https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/koutou/shikaku/07111316.htm>
- Notes: Elementary, junior high and senior high school in China usually add up to 12 years, and 16 years after a four-year bachelor's degree. This is counted on the common school system; if your system was different, count for yourself. A three-year junior college (大专) adds up to 15 years. Whether that is enough, and whether an individual examination is needed, is judged by the graduate school you apply to. This section draws no conclusion. The pages also list other qualifications, such as three-year bachelor's programs, which this section does not go into. Having the admission qualification only means you can apply. Whether you are admitted is a separate matter.

### 7. When choosing a professional training college, look at two numbers: only a course of 2 years or more and 1,700 class hours or more gives the Diploma, and only with it can you connect to university transfer admission and to the status of residence for job-hunting after graduation
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Before enrolling, ask the school whether this course confers the Diploma.
- In plain words: Only a professional training college course with a course term of 2 years or more and 1,700 total class hours or more confers the title of Diploma (専門士) on graduation, and only then do you have the qualification for transfer admission to a university. A course of 4 years or more and 3,400 class hours or more confers the Advanced Diploma (高度専門士). For the status of residence that lets you stay in Japan after graduation to keep looking for a job, what is required on the professional training college side is also having obtained the Diploma. The qualification for admission to a graduate school is given only for courses separately designated by the Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology.
- Benefit: MEXT's page states that the requirements for a course that confers the title 「専門士」 ("Diploma") are 「修業年限が2年以上」 ("a course term of 2 years or more"), 「総授業時数が1,700 単位時間( 62 単位）以上」 ("total class hours of 1,700 unit hours (62 credits) or more") and so on. These requirements are largely the same as the requirements for a course recognized for transfer admission to a university, so a person who completes such a course is recognized as qualified for transfer admission to a university. The requirements for 「高度専門士」 ("Advanced Diploma") are 「修業年限が4年以上」 ("a course term of 4 years or more"), 「総授業時数が3,400 単位時間（124 単位）以上」 ("total class hours of 3,400 unit hours (124 credits) or more") and so on. The page also says: 「大学院への入学資格については、文部科学大臣が別に指定する専修学校の専門課程を修了した者に与えられ認められます。」 ("The qualification for admission to a graduate school is given to and recognized for a person who has completed a post-secondary course of a specialized training college separately designated by the Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology.") On the ISA's page about continuing job-hunting after graduation, the people covered are written as graduates of a university or of an advanced course (専攻科) of a specialized training college, and international students and others who graduated 「専修学校専門課程において専門士の称号を取得して」 ("having obtained the title of Diploma in a post-secondary course of a specialized training college").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 文部科学省. 専門士・高度専門士の称号とは. <https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shougai/senshuu/1382378.htm>；出入国在留管理庁. 大学等を卒業後就職活動のための滞在をご希望のみなさまへ. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/nyukan_nyukan84.html>
- Notes: When a professional training college graduate applies for a work-type status of residence, for how closely what you studied must relate to the work, see Item 13 (look for work that matches your major). A 1-year course does not give the Diploma. For whether the Diploma can be evaluated as a credential in China, see Item 11 (the evaluation in China does not accept language-school completion certificates, preparatory-course certificates or research-student records).

### 8. Privately financed international students with good grades and small remittances from home can receive the Honors Scholarship: 48,000 yen a month at university and graduate school
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. You must keep your grades above the standard.
- In plain words: This is a grant paid to privately financed international students. It is 48,000 yen a month at graduate and undergraduate level, and 30,000 yen a month at Japanese-language institutions. The conditions include: your status of residence is Student; your grade evaluation coefficient for the previous fiscal year is 2.30 or higher and you can keep it there; and the money sent from home (excluding the admission fee and tuition) averages no more than 90,000 yen a month.
- Benefit: JASSO's page on the Honors Scholarship for Privately-Financed International Students (学習奨励費) states that the program covers privately financed foreign students enrolled at a university, graduate school, junior college, college of technology, post-secondary course of a specialized training college, preparatory education course or Japanese-language institution. The amounts are 「大学院レベル・学部レベル：月額48,000円」 ("graduate level and undergraduate level: 48,000 yen per month") and 「日本語教育機関：月額30,000円」 ("Japanese-language institutions: 30,000 yen per month"). The definition of a privately financed foreign student states that it is a person who holds the status of residence 「留学」 ("Student"), and that it does not include Japanese government scholarship students or students dispatched by a foreign government. The conditions for those covered include: the grade evaluation coefficient for the previous fiscal year is 「2.30以上」 ("2.30 or higher") at every level and is expected to be kept during the payment period; and remittances (excluding the admission fee, tuition and the like) are 「平均月額90,000円以下」 ("90,000 yen or less per month on average").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 日本学生支援機構. 留学生受入れ促進プログラム(文部科学省外国人留学生学習奨励費)について. <https://www.jasso.go.jp/ryugaku/scholarship_j/shoreihi/about.html>
- Notes: The page lists more conditions than these. This section read only the ones above. This section did not verify item by item how to apply, whether it is only possible through a recommendation from the school you are enrolled in, how many places there are, or how long it is paid. The amounts follow the page's current wording; this section did not verify from which fiscal year the figures apply. There is a separate reservation program for people with good EJU scores, which this section did not read. Other scholarships (Japanese government, JSPS, local government, private foundations, the school's own reductions) each have their own conditions, and this item does not speak for them. They are collected in the [scholarship overview](../docs/scholarship-overview.md).

### 9. If you have graduated and not yet found a job, you can change to a status of residence for job-hunting, 6 months each time, normally one year in total
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: One fee for the change and one for the extension; see Section 1, Item 9 (fees for extensions, changes and permanent residence). You need a recommendation from the school you graduated from.
- In plain words: This is for graduates of a university or of an advanced course of a specialized training college, and for people who graduated with the Diploma. If your period of stay as a Student runs out and you want to stay and keep looking for a job, and your residence record has no problems and the school you graduated from recommends you, you can change to Designated Activities (特定活動). The period is 6 months, and you can extend once, for a total of one year after graduation. If you qualify for an eligible local-government job-hunting support program, you can stay one more year in the second year. If you already have a job offer (内定) and there is still some time before you start, there is a separate Designated Activities status for people with a job offer.
- Benefit: The ISA's page says the following. Where an international student or the like who graduated from a university or an advanced course of a specialized training college, or who graduated having obtained the title of Diploma in a post-secondary course of a specialized training college, wishes to stay on in Japan for job-hunting after the period of stay as 「留学」 ("Student") expires, and where the residence record has no problems, there is a recommendation from the educational institution graduated from, and so on, a change to a status of residence for job-hunting is permitted, 「（特定活動、在留期間は６月）」 ("(Designated Activities, with a period of stay of 6 months)"), and one further extension of the period of stay is permitted. So you can stay in Japan for 1 year after graduation for job-hunting. In the 2nd year after graduation, a person who becomes covered by a qualifying job-hunting support program of a local government and obtains its certificate can stay 1 more year. Another page states that a person who gets a job offer while enrolled or while continuing job-hunting, and who wishes to stay in Japan until being taken on by the company, can, if certain conditions are met, change to 「特定活動」 ("Designated Activities") for the purpose of staying until the time of hiring.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁. 大学等を卒業後就職活動のための滞在をご希望のみなさまへ. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/nyukan_nyukan84.html>；出入国在留管理庁. 大学又は専門学校の在学中又は卒業後に就職先が内定し採用までの滞在をご希望のみなさまへ. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/nyuukokukanri07_00013.html>
- Notes: Language school graduates are generally not covered by this item. The exception is a person who graduated from a university outside Japan, holds a bachelor's degree or higher, and studied at a Japanese-language institution that meets the conditions. The conditions listed on the page include attendance at the Japanese-language institution of 「おおむね９割以上」 ("roughly 90 percent or more") and the ability to pay expenses. This section did not verify whether you can work part-time during this period or what separate permission you would need. Whether the school gives you a recommendation letter depends on your attendance and grades while enrolled. This is the common account, and there is no original text for it. For the specific conditions of the Designated Activities status for people with a job offer, this section read only the outline.

### 10. If you start work in April, apply between December 1 and the end of January to change from Student to a work status
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Applying costs nothing, but you pay a fee when permission is granted; see Section 1, Item 9 (fees for extension, change of status and permanent residence). Have your documents ready before December.
- In plain words: Applications are heavily concentrated in January to March each year. The ISA asks people who plan to start work in April to file the application for change between December 1 and the end of January. If documents are missing or you file late, the examination may not be finished by the day you want.

### 12. A language school is at most 2 years; both notified (告示校) and accredited (認定校) schools require 80% monthly attendance; below 50%, a notified school reports you to Immigration and an accredited school records where you work part-time
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. The price is attending enough classes every month.
- In plain words: You can normally be enrolled at a Japanese-language institution for at most 2 years. The rules for notified schools and accredited schools are in two different documents, but the 80% line is the same: if your attendance in a month falls below 80%, the school must guide you until it is back to 80% and keep a record, except for absence due to illness or other unavoidable reasons. The 50% line differs: a notified school must report you to Immigration by the end of the following month, including the name of your part-time employer if you have permission to work; an accredited school, in addition to guidance, confirms and records your employer. An average six-month attendance rate below 70% across all students is one example Immigration gives of a school being "inappropriate", which affects the school and therefore your renewal. To go on to higher education in Japan after graduating, you need Japanese at B2 or above.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency's page for prospective students states: 「日本語教育機関に在籍できる期間は、通常、最長２年間です。」 ("The period for which you can be enrolled at a Japanese-language institution is normally a maximum of 2 years.") The same page requires Japanese at B2 or above on the 「日本語教育の参照枠」 (Japanese-language education reference framework) for going on to a university or specialized training college. For notified schools, Article 1, paragraph 1, item 37 of the 『日本語教育機関の告示基準』 (Public Notice Standards for Japanese-Language Institutions) requires that for a student whose monthly attendance 「が８割を下回った留学生については、１か月の出席率が８割以上になるまで改善のための指導を行い、その指導の状況を記録する」 ("has fallen below 80%, guidance for improvement is given until the monthly attendance rate is 80% or more, and the guidance is recorded"); item 38 requires that when a student withdraws the school 「その翌月末までに地方出入国在留管理局に対し当該留学生について報告する」 ("reports that student to the Regional Immigration Services Bureau by the end of the following month"); item 39 requires that a student whose monthly attendance falls below 50% be reported to the Regional Immigration Services Bureau by the end of the following month, together with the name of the organization where they work if they hold permission for activities outside their status. For accredited schools, the 『出席管理及び在留継続支援体制に係る認定日本語教育機関の運営に関するガイドライン』 (Guidelines on the operation of accredited Japanese-language institutions concerning attendance management and support systems for continued residence), set by the Immigration Services Agency and MEXT on 12 April 2024, states that it sets out the 「実施が求められる事項」 ("matters required to be implemented") under Articles 30 and 34 of the accreditation standards and serves as the standard when the Minister of Justice confirms facts and when the Minister of Education requests reports. Item 1(2): 「１か月の出席率（その月に出席した単位時間数を出席すべき単位時間数で除した数をいう。）が８割を下回った生徒（疾病その他のやむを得ない事由により欠席した者を除く。）については、１か月の出席率が８割以上になるまで改善のための指導を行い、その指導の状況を記録すること」 ("for a student whose monthly attendance rate (the number of unit hours attended in the month divided by the number that should have been attended) has fallen below 80%, excluding those absent due to illness or other unavoidable reasons, give guidance for improvement until the monthly attendance rate is 80% or more, and record that guidance"). Item 1(3): 「１か月の出席率が５割を下回った生徒（疾病その他のやむを得ない事由により欠席した者を除く。）については、上記（２）による指導等に加え、当該生徒が資格外活動の許可を受けている場合は当該許可に係る活動を行う本邦の公私の機関の名称を確認し、記録すること」 ("for a student whose monthly attendance rate has fallen below 50%, excluding those absent due to illness or other unavoidable reasons, in addition to the guidance under (2), where the student holds permission for activities outside their status, confirm and record the name of the organization in Japan where the permitted activity is carried out"). Item 2(8): when a student withdraws or is removed from the register, 「各種法令等に従い、適切に報告することとすること」 ("report appropriately in accordance with the relevant laws and regulations"). Note 2 gives as examples of 「学習活動を適正に行っているとは認められない生徒が相当数存在する場合」 ("a case where a considerable number of students are not recognized as properly carrying out learning activities"): 「全ての生徒の６か月間出席率の平均が７割を下回ること」 ("the average six-month attendance rate of all students falls below 70%") and 「一暦年中に入学した者の３割以上が、在留期間の更新又は変更を受けないで在留期間を経過して本邦に在留するに至ったこと」 ("30% or more of those admitted in a calendar year come to remain in Japan beyond their period of stay without renewal or change of status"). Article 30 of the Accreditation Standards for Accredited Japanese-Language Institutions requires an accredited school 「授業への出席率が低い生徒に対する必要な指導を行うために必要な体制を整備しなければならない」 ("to establish the system necessary to give necessary guidance to students with low class attendance"); Article 34 requires an accredited school with a course for study abroad to establish a system to support students in residing properly and continuing their studies; Article 17, paragraph 1 provides that the period of study of a course for study abroad 「一年以上でなければならない」 ("must be 1 year or more"), and may be 6 months or more in special circumstances.

- Notes: Both documents are requirements on the school, not rules for penalizing students. The accredited school's 50% rule says only "confirm and record"; it does not say report to Immigration. Which authority the school reports what to, item 2(8) of the guidelines says only 「各種法令等に従い」, and which specific law was not verified in this section. How Immigration handles a report, and whether it affects renewal, neither document says, and this was not verified. ### 13. If you study at a professional training college, look for work that matches your major: immigration requires a "considerable degree" of relevance for specialized training colleges
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Keep this in mind both when you choose a major and when you apply for jobs.
- In plain words: When you apply for a work status such as Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services (技術・人文知識・国際業務), immigration looks at whether what you majored in relates to the work you will do. For university graduates this is judged fairly flexibly. For graduates of specialized training colleges, a considerable degree of relevance is required in principle. Graduates of courses accredited by the Minister of Education are considered separately. Even where the major does not match directly, the whole of what you studied may be looked at in an overall judgment.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency's guideline requires that you majored in, and graduated in, subjects related to the skills or knowledge needed for the work. For universities: 「大学における専攻科目と従事しようとする業務の関連性については、従来より柔軟に判断しています」 ("the relevance between the major at a university and the intended work has long been judged flexibly"). For specialized training colleges, because their purpose is to develop abilities needed for an occupation or for practical life: 「原則として専修学校における専攻科目と従事しようとする業務については、相当程度の関連性を必要とします」 ("in principle, a considerable degree of relevance is required between the major at a specialized training college and the intended work"). The guideline goes on to say that people who completed a post-secondary course or advanced course accredited by the Minister under the 「外国人留学生キャリア形成促進プログラム」 (Career Development Promotion Program for International Students) are considered separately, and that where a person who completed a post-secondary or advanced course cannot be found to have directly majored in considerably related subjects, an overall judgment is made if the whole of the coursework shows that the related knowledge was acquired. It also states that people who completed a post-secondary course of a specialized training college must specifically meet requirements such as being entitled to use the title Diploma (専門士).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁. 「技術・人文知識・国際業務」の在留資格の明確化等について（平成 20 年 3 月策定、最終改定令和 8 年 4 月）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/001413639.pdf>
- Notes: The guideline gives no ratio for how much "a considerable degree" is. It depends on the individual case, and this section draws no conclusion. How much more flexibly graduates of accredited courses are treated was read only as far as "considered separately", not in full. The guideline is immigration's own interpretation, not statutory text, and it is revised fairly often; the version read here is the one last revised in April 2026. Read this item after Item 7 (the Diploma): holding the Diploma only gets you through the first gate.
