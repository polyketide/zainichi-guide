# 1. Residence Status

This section is about your personal freedom and your money. In Japan, your status of residence (在留資格) comes before everything else: work, renting, bank accounts and pension all hang on it. What goes wrong is usually not something big. It is a missed 14-day notification (届出), or an application filed late.

This section is written for mid- to long-term residents (中長期在留者), meaning people who hold a residence card (在留カード). For the law, it uses the current text of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act (出入国管理及び難民認定法, "the Immigration Control Act") and its Enforcement Regulation. For procedures and amounts, it uses the website of the Immigration Services Agency (出入国在留管理庁, "the ISA"). Everything is as of 2026-10-05, and each amount states the date from which it applies. This section does not answer whether an individual case will be approved. For that, ask immigration or a gyoseishoshi (行政書士, administrative scrivener).

Order: first come the notifications and deadlines, which keep you clear of penalties if you simply follow them; among them, the extension in Item 1 carries a fee. In the middle are part-time work and leaving Japan. Then come fees, permanent residence and application documents, and the last item is where to ask when unsure.

### 1. Apply to extend your period of stay from 3 months before it expires, not in the last week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: For the fee, see Item 9 (fees for extensions and changes). A 1-year period applied for at the counter is 33,000 yen. Preparing the documents plus one trip takes a few hours.
- In plain words: If your period of stay is 6 months or longer, you can apply for an extension from 3 months before it expires. If you file before the expiry date, you can lawfully stay up to 2 more months even if the result has not come out. If you do not file before the expiry date, you are overstaying from the next day, and that can bring a prison sentence.
- Benefit: The ISA gives the application period as 「在留期間の満了する日以前（６か月以上の在留期間を有する者にあっては在留期間の満了する３か月前から。）」 ("on or before the day the period of stay expires (for a person with a period of stay of 6 months or more, from 3 months before the period of stay expires)"). If you filed before the expiry date and no result has come by that date, Article 20, paragraph (6) of the Immigration Control Act lets you keep staying in Japan under your previous status of residence until the moment the result comes out, or until the day 2 months after the original expiry date, whichever is earlier. Under Article 21, paragraph (4), the same applies to applications for extension of the period of stay (在留期間更新). This paragraph does not apply to a person whose period of stay is 30 days or less. For a person who neither extended nor changed status and stays past the deadline, the penalty in Article 70, paragraph (1), item (v) is 「三年以下の拘禁刑若しくは三百万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 3 years or a fine of not more than 3,000,000 yen"), and both can be imposed together.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法（昭和二十六年政令第三百十九号）第二十条第六項、第二十一条第四項、第七十条第一項第五号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>；出入国在留管理庁. 在留期間更新許可申請. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/16-3.html>
- Notes: For cases such as study abroad, a long overseas posting, childbirth or hospitalization, the ISA states that it can accept the application earlier than 3 months before. Take your documents and ask first. The extra 2 months exist only when "the application was already filed before the expiry date". This section did not verify against the original text whether you can leave Japan and come back as usual during those 2 months. Ask immigration before you travel.

### 2. Register your new address at the municipal office within 14 days of moving, and bring your residence card
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. One trip to the municipal office (市区町村役所), with your residence card.
- In plain words: Within 14 days of moving, you must take your residence card to the municipal office of your new address and register. If you hand your residence card to the municipal office when you file the moving-in notification for your resident record (住民票) (the statute says 「提出して」, "submitting", not showing), that counts as reporting to immigration at the same time. Not reporting carries a fine of up to 200,000 yen. If you still have not reported 90 days after moving out, your status of residence can be revoked.
- Benefit: Article 19-9, paragraph (1) of the Immigration Control Act requires a mid- to long-term resident to make a notification at the municipality of the new address, bringing the residence card, 「十四日以内」 ("within 14 days") from the day of moving to the new address. Paragraph (3) of the same article states that a person who files a moving-in or change-of-address notification 「在留カードを提出して」 ("submitting the residence card") under the Basic Resident Registration Act (住民基本台帳法) is deemed to have made this notification. For failing to notify, Article 71-5, item (ii) provides 「二十万円以下の罰金」 ("a fine of not more than 200,000 yen"). For notifying a false address, Article 71-2, item (i) provides 「一年以下の拘禁刑又は二十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 1 year or a fine of not more than 200,000 yen"). Article 22-4, paragraph (1), item (ix) lists failing to notify the new address within 90 days of leaving the old one as a ground on which the status of residence can be revoked, except where there is a justifiable reason. Notifying a false address is item (x) of the same paragraph.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第十九条の九、第二十二条の四第一項第九号・第十号、第七十一条の二第一号、第七十一条の五第二号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>
- Notes: For a person who has just entered Japan, it is within 14 days from the day the address is settled (Article 19-7). Late reporting also goes into the "public obligations" part of the permanent residence examination; see Item 10 (if you want permanent residence).

### 3. Notify immigration within 14 days each time you leave a job, start a job or change organizations; you can do it online
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. You fill in the ISA's electronic notification system once, which takes ten-odd minutes. You must register before first use.
- In plain words: When a contract ends, when you sign a new one, when you graduate from or leave a school, or when your organization changes its name or address, you must report to immigration within 14 days. Leaving a job and starting a job are two separate events. You can report them separately, or together on one form. Not reporting carries a fine of up to 200,000 yen.
- Benefit: Article 19-16 of the Immigration Control Act requires a notification 「十四日以内」 ("within 14 days") from the day the event occurs. It has two categories. Professor, Student, Business Manager, Highly Skilled Professional (i)(c) and others report a change of name or location of the organization of activity (活動機関), its extinction, or 「離脱若しくは移籍」 ("leaving or transferring"). Researcher, Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services, Highly Skilled Professional (i)(a) and (i)(b), Skilled Labor, Specified Skilled Worker and others report a change of name or location of the contracting organization (契約機関), its extinction, or 「契約の終了若しくは新たな契約の締結」 ("the termination of the contract or the conclusion of a new contract"). The ISA's explanation reads the latter category as changing jobs and leaving a job. A person with Dependent status (as a spouse), Spouse or Child of Japanese National, or Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident must also report a divorce from the spouse or the spouse's death. For failing to notify, Article 71-5, item (iii) provides 「二十万円以下の罰金」 ("a fine of not more than 200,000 yen"). A false notification falls under Article 71-2, item (i): 「一年以下の拘禁刑又は二十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 1 year or a fine of not more than 200,000 yen"). There are three ways to notify: online, at the counter, and by post. The ISA writes 「インターネットによる届出が便利です」 ("notification via the internet is convenient"), and an online notification needs no supporting documents.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第十九条の十六、第七十一条の二第一号、第七十一条の五第三号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>；出入国在留管理庁. 所属（契約）機関に関する届出. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/nyuukokukanri10_00015.html>；出入国在留管理庁. 所属（活動）機関に関する届出. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/nyuukokukanri10_00014.html>
- Notes: Permanent residents are not covered by this item. If you notify by post, immigration sends no receipt, and the official advice is to use a mailing method that leaves a delivery record. Late reporting also goes into the permanent residence examination; see Item 10 (if you want permanent residence).

### 4. Do not stay without a job for 3 months or more after leaving: without a justifiable reason, your status of residence can be revoked
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. The price is that you must work out your next step before you leave the job, and keep a record of your job search.
- In plain words: If you hold a work-type or study-type status of residence and you go 3 months or more in a row without doing the activity that status is for, immigration can revoke the status. It does not count if you have a justifiable reason. If immigration does move to revoke, it must first hear your explanation, and after revocation it gives you up to 30 days to leave Japan.
- Benefit: The ground for revocation listed in Article 22-4, paragraph (1), item (vi) of the Immigration Control Act is this: a person holding a status of residence in Appended Table I stays in Japan without engaging in the corresponding activity for 「継続して三月」 ("three months continuously") or more. For Highly Skilled Professional (ii) it is 6 months. The parentheses in the same item state 「正当な理由がある場合を除く」 ("except where there is a justifiable reason"). Item (v) of the same paragraph is a different case: the person is not engaging in the original activity, and is engaging in or intends to engage in another activity. On procedure, paragraph (2) requires an immigration inspector to hear the person's opinion before revocation. Paragraph (3) requires prior service of a written notice stating the date, place and cause. Paragraph (4) allows the person or a representative to appear, state an opinion and submit evidence. Where the status is revoked on a ground other than items (i) and (ii), paragraph (7) requires a period for departure to be designated 「三十日を超えない範囲内」 ("within a limit not exceeding 30 days"). The exception is a revocation under item (v) where there are reasonable grounds to suspect the person will flee.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第二十二条の四第一項第五号・第六号、第二項から第四項まで、第七項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>
- Notes: The provision says the status "can be revoked". It does not lapse automatically at 3 months. The provision does not list what counts as a justifiable reason. This section did not verify the original text of the ISA's interpretation, so it gives no examples. Item (vi) does not apply to the statuses based on personal status or position in Appended Table II (Permanent Resident, Spouse or Child of Japanese National, Long-Term Resident). The job departure itself must first be reported under Item 3 (notify immigration within 14 days each time you leave a job, start a job).

### 5. Carry your residence card at all times, and show it when the police ask
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Put the card in your wallet when you go out.
- In plain words: Mid- to long-term residents aged 16 or over must carry their residence card at all times. Not carrying it brings a fine of up to 200,000 yen. Refusing to show it when a police officer or immigration officer on duty asks can bring a sentence of up to 1 year or a fine of up to 200,000 yen. The officer also has a duty to show their own identification, and you can ask to see it.
- Benefit: Article 23, paragraph (2) of the Immigration Control Act says of the residence card that a mid- to long-term resident 「常にこれを携帯していなければならない」 ("must carry it at all times"). Paragraph (3) requires you to comply when an immigration inspector, immigration control officer, police officer, coast guard officer or similar official asks you to present it in the course of duty. Paragraph (4) requires these officials to carry identification, and 「請求があるときは、これを提示しなければならない」 ("they must present it when requested"). Paragraph (5) exempts persons under 16 from the duty to carry. For not carrying the card, Article 75-3 provides 「二十万円以下の罰金」 ("a fine of not more than 200,000 yen"). For refusing to present it, Article 75-2, item (ii) provides 「一年以下の拘禁刑又は二十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 1 year or a fine of not more than 200,000 yen").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第二十三条第二項から第五項まで、第七十五条の二、第七十五条の三. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>
- Notes: The provision does not say whether carrying a photocopy or a photo on your phone counts as "carrying". This section draws no conclusion. Act on the basis that you carry the original.

### 6. If you lose your residence card, apply for a replacement at immigration within 14 days of noticing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. One trip to immigration, half a day.
- In plain words: If your residence card is lost, stolen or destroyed, you must go to immigration and apply for a replacement within 14 days from the day you notice. If you notice while abroad, the count starts from the day you return to Japan. Not applying can bring a sentence of up to 1 year or a fine of up to 200,000 yen.
- Benefit: Article 19-12, paragraph (1) of the Immigration Control Act provides that a person who no longer has the residence card because of loss, theft, destruction or the like must apply for reissuance 「十四日以内」 ("within 14 days") from the day of learning of it. If the person learns of it while outside Japan, the count starts from the day of the first entry into Japan after that. For a violation, Article 71-2, item (ii) provides 「一年以下の拘禁刑又は二十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 1 year or a fine of not more than 200,000 yen"). The ISA page 「紛失等による在留カードの再交付申請」 (application for reissue of a residence card due to loss and the like) gives the application period as 「当該事実を知った日（本邦から出国している間に当該事実を知った場合は、その後最初に入国した日）から１４日以内」 ("within 14 days from the day you learn of the fact, or, if you learn of it while outside Japan, from the day of your first entry afterwards") and, under fees, 「手数料はかかりません」 ("no fee is charged"); past 14 days, a separate document stating the reason is required.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第十九条の十二第一項、第七十一条の二第二号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>；出入国在留管理庁. 紛失等による在留カードの再交付申請. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/nyuukokukanri10_00010.html>
- Notes: This section read only the application-period and fee fields of the ISA's procedure page and did not go through the list of documents to bring when applying (for example, a police certificate that a loss report was accepted). Before you go, call the number in Item 12 (the immigration information line) and ask.

### 7. Get permission for activities outside your status before taking part-time work, and keep within 28 hours a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. The price is keeping one combined record of the hours of all your jobs.
- In plain words: Statuses such as Student and Dependent do not themselves allow part-time work. You must get permission first. The permission is capped at 28 hours a week. During the long vacations set by their school, students can work up to 8 hours a day. You cannot work at adult entertainment businesses. Working without permission can bring a sentence of up to 1 year or a fine of up to 2 million yen, and a person whose main activity is that work can get up to 3 years.
- Benefit: Article 19, paragraph (1) of the Immigration Control Act prohibits a person holding a status of residence in Appended Table I from engaging in income-earning activities outside that status. Paragraph (2) provides that the person can apply for permission (資格外活動許可, permission to engage in an activity other than that permitted under the status of residence previously granted). Article 19, paragraph (5), item (i) of the Enforcement Regulation gives the content of the permission as 「一週について二十八時間以内」 ("within 28 hours per week"). For a person with Student status, during the long vacation periods set by the school rules of the educational institution, it is 「一日について八時間以内」 ("within 8 hours per day"). The same item excludes activities at places of entertainment and amusement business (風俗営業), store-based sex-related special business and the like. It also states that a student's permission is valid only 「教育機関に在籍している間」 ("while enrolled at the educational institution"). For a violation of Article 19, paragraph (1), Article 73, item (i) provides 「一年以下の拘禁刑若しくは二百万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 1 year or a fine of not more than 2,000,000 yen"), and both can be imposed together. For a person clearly found to be engaging 「専ら」 ("solely") in such activities, Article 70, paragraph (1), item (iv) provides 「三年以下の拘禁刑若しくは三百万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 3 years or a fine of not more than 3,000,000 yen"). For an employer who has a foreign national work illegally, Article 73-2 provides not more than three years or not more than three million yen.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第十九条第一項・第二項、第七十条第一項第四号、第七十三条、第七十三条の二. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>；出入国管理及び難民認定法施行規則（昭和五十六年法務省令第五十四号）第十九条第五項第一号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/356M50000010054>
- Notes: After graduating or leaving school, a student can no longer use this permission, even if the period on the residence card has not run out. As to whether the hours of several jobs are added together, the original text of the regulation speaks of the total for one week; this section did not verify the original text of the ISA's Q&A. A person with a work-type status who takes on work outside the main job is also within the scope of Article 19. Exceptions such as honoraria for lectures are set by Ministry of Justice order, and this section does not go into them. The employer penalty is read from the text currently in force. This section did not check whether an amendment raising it has been promulgated but is not yet in force.

### 8. Get a re-entry permit before leaving Japan for more than 1 year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: A short trip abroad costs nothing. If you will be away for more than 1 year, go to immigration before you leave and get a re-entry permit (再入国許可). The fee is 4,000 yen for a single re-entry permit and 7,000 yen for a multiple re-entry permit, each 500 yen less if applied for online.
- In plain words: If you leave Japan with a valid passport and residence card and indicate at the airport that you will come back, you are automatically treated as having a re-entry permit. This permit lasts at most 1 year. If your period of stay has less than 1 year left, the period of stay is the limit. It cannot be extended from abroad. To be away longer, you must get a formal re-entry permit before you leave, valid for at most 5 years.
- Benefit: Article 26-2, paragraph (1) of the Immigration Control Act provides that a person who holds a status of residence, a valid passport and a residence card, and who expresses to an immigration inspector at departure the intention to re-enter, is deemed to have obtained a re-entry permit (the special re-entry permit, みなし再入国許可). Paragraph (2) provides that this permit is valid for 1 year from the day of departure. If the period of stay expires within that 1 year, it is valid until the period of stay expires. Paragraph (3) provides that Article 26, paragraph (5) does not apply to it, and that paragraph is precisely the rule on applying from abroad to extend the validity period. For the formal re-entry permit, Article 26, paragraph (3) provides a validity period 「五年を超えない範囲内」 ("within a limit not exceeding 5 years"). Paragraph (5) provides that a person who is abroad and has reasonable grounds for being unable to return can apply for an extension of not more than 1 year, and not beyond 6 years from the day the permit took effect. Article 25, paragraph (1), item (iii) of the Enforcement Order of the Immigration Control Act sets the fee for a re-entry permit (other than a multiple re-entry permit) at 「四千円（当該許可の申請が電子申請により行われた場合にあっては、三千五百円）」 ("4,000 yen, or 3,500 yen where the application is made electronically"), and item (iv) sets the multiple re-entry permit at 「七千円（当該許可の申請が電子申請により行われた場合にあっては、六千五百円）」 ("7,000 yen, or 6,500 yen where the application is made electronically").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第二十六条第三項・第五項、第二十六条の二. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>；出入国管理及び難民認定法施行令（平成十年政令第百七十八号）第二十五条第一項第三号・第四号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/410CO0000000178>
- Notes: This item applies equally to permanent residents. The fees are those in the text of the Enforcement Order in force from 1 October 2026; the October 2026 fee-revision poster has no entry for re-entry permits. This section did not verify the original text of the ISA's explanation of what happens to the status of residence after the permit expires. It states only the time limits, not the consequences. Whether a long absence from Japan affects the counting of "continuous residence" in a permanent residence application was also not verified against the original text.

### 9. Fees for extensions, changes and permanent residence rose sharply on October 1, 2026, and are charged by the length of stay granted
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: This item itself costs nothing. Knowing the amounts lets you set the money aside in advance.
- In plain words: For applications filed on or after October 1, 2026, the fee for an extension or a change of status is charged by the length of stay that is granted. A 1-year period applied for at the counter is 33,000 yen. 3 years or more and under 5 years is 64,000 yen. 5 years or more is 75,000 yen. Except for the shortest tier, applying online is cheaper by several thousand to ten thousand yen. Permission for permanent residence is 200,000 yen.
- Benefit: The revised amounts published by the ISA are as follows. The first figure is for a counter application and the second for an online application. Permitted period of 3 months or less: 10,000 yen, 10,000 yen. Over 3 months up to 6 months: 18,000 yen, 15,000 yen. Over 6 months and under 1 year: 25,000 yen, 21,000 yen. 1 year: 33,000 yen, 27,000 yen. Over 1 year and under 3 years: 48,000 yen, 42,000 yen. 3 years or more and under 5 years: 64,000 yen, 56,000 yen. 5 years or more: 75,000 yen, 65,000 yen. Permission for permanent residence is counter only, 200,000 yen. Before the revision, changes and extensions were 6,000 yen at the counter and 5,500 yen online, and permanent residence was 10,000 yen. The new amounts apply to people who file an application on or after October 1, 2026 and are granted permission. An application accepted on or before September 30 is charged the old amount even if it is approved on or after October 1. People who meet certain conditions can get a reduction, and the extension page states that the reduced fee is 10,000 yen whatever the length of stay. Counter applications are paid with revenue stamps (収入印紙). Online applications can be paid only at a convenience store or by bank payment.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁. 令和８年１０月１日付け在留許可手数料の額の改定等について. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/01_00644.html>；出入国在留管理庁. ポスター（日本語版）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/001469198.pdf>；出入国在留管理庁. 在留期間更新許可申請. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/16-3.html>
- Notes: The fee is paid when you receive the permission. If it is not granted, you do not pay. Who qualifies for the reduction depends on a separate ISA page, the guide to persons eligible for the reduction, which this section did not read. The online amounts are followed by a "※" mark stating that there are other costs besides the fee. That sentence was cut off in the file obtained, and what it says was not verified. ### 10. If you want permanent residence, start now: pay tax, pension and health insurance on time, and file every notification on time
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No extra money. You have to pay these anyway. The price is that you cannot be late. You can set them up as automatic bank debits.
- In plain words: Permanent residence in principle requires 10 continuous years of living in Japan, including 5 years or more on a work-type or residence-type status. Tax, pension, health insurance and immigration notifications must all be done on time. Paying late and making it up afterwards still counts against you in principle. Highly skilled professionals can apply after 3 years with 70 points or more, or after 1 year with 80 points or more.
- Benefit: The conditions in Article 22, paragraph (2) of the Immigration Control Act are good conduct, assets or skills sufficient for an independent living, and that permanent residence is in the interests of Japan. The ISA's guidelines break the third condition (that permanent residence is in the interests of Japan) down into several points. First, 「原則として引き続き１０年以上本邦に在留していること」 ("in principle, having resided in Japan continuously for 10 years or more"), including 「引き続き５年以上」 ("continuously for 5 years or more") on a work status or a residence status; Technical Intern Training and Specified Skilled Worker (i) do not count. Second, not having been sentenced to a fine, imprisonment or the like, and properly fulfilling public obligations. The guidelines list payment of taxes, of contributions for public pension and public medical insurance, and obligations such as the notifications required by the Immigration Control Act. A note in the guidelines states: 「申請時点において納税（納付）済みであったとしても、当初の納税（納付）期間内に履行されていない場合は、原則として消極的に評価されます」 ("even if the tax (contribution) has been paid by the time of application, where it was not paid within the original payment period, this is in principle evaluated negatively"). Third, the current status of residence carries the longest period of stay. Until March 31 of Reiwa 9, a period of stay of "3 years" is also treated as the longest. Among the exceptions to the 10 years: a person with 70 points or more under the points calculation in the Highly Skilled Professional ministerial order who has resided continuously for 3 years or more can apply, and for 80 points or more it is continuously for 1 year or more. For the spouse of a Japanese national or of a permanent resident, it is a real marriage that has lasted 3 years or more, plus continuous residence of 1 year or more.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第二十二条第二項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>；出入国在留管理庁. 永住許可に関するガイドライン（令和８年２月２４日改訂）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/nyukan_nyukan50.html>
- Notes: The guidelines are only factors considered in the examination. Meeting them does not guarantee approval. "The notifications required by the Immigration Control Act" are the ones in Item 2 (within 14 days of moving) and Item 3 (within 14 days each time you leave a job, start a job), so the price of reporting late under those two items is more than the 200,000 yen fine. The transitional treatment of "3 years as the longest" runs until March 31, 2027. How it is counted after that depends on the original text of Note 1 of the guidelines. For the permanent residence fee, see Item 9 (fees for extensions, changes and permanent residence).

### 11. Submit only genuine application documents; do not let an agent "prepare" documents for you
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain words: Getting permission with false documents or by other wrongful means can bring a sentence of up to 3 years or a fine of up to 3,000,000 yen, and the status of residence can also be revoked. A person who helps someone do this for profit can likewise get up to 3 years. The only people who can go to immigration and submit documents for you are attorneys and gyoseishoshi who have notified immigration, and a few other categories of people.
- Benefit: Article 70, paragraph (1), item (ii)-2 of the Immigration Control Act punishes a person who obtains landing permission, or a permission under Chapter IV, Section 2, 「偽りその他不正の手段により」 ("by deceit or other wrongful means"). Permission for a change of status, an extension and permanent residence are all in that section. The penalty is 「三年以下の拘禁刑若しくは三百万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 3 years or a fine of not more than 3,000,000 yen"), and both can be imposed together. Article 22-4, paragraph (1), item (ii) lists obtaining permission by wrongful means as a ground for revoking the status of residence, and item (iii) is submitting documents containing false statements. Article 74-6 punishes a person who, for profit, makes these acts easier to carry out, with the same penalty. The ISA's extension application page lists the people who can submit an application on your behalf: your legal representative; staff of your organization or school and the like who are approved by the director of the regional immigration bureau; and an attorney or gyoseishoshi who has notified the director of the regional immigration bureau. Only when you are under 16 or cannot appear because of illness can a relative or a person living with you act for you. The page states that 「仕事の多忙や通勤・通学等」 ("being busy at work, commuting to work or school, and the like") is not a reason for being unable to appear.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第二十二条の四第一項第二号・第三号、第七十条第一項第二号の二、第七十四条の六. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>；出入国在留管理庁. 在留期間更新許可申請. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/16-3.html>
- Notes: A person whose status is revoked under Article 22-4, paragraph (1), item (i) or item (ii) is given no period to prepare for departure. Staying in Japan after the revocation is the offense in Article 70, paragraph (1), item (iii).

### 12. When unsure about a procedure, call the immigration information line first; do not rely on what people say in chat groups
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: The cost of the call. Call on a weekday in the daytime. You may need several tries to get through.
- In plain words: The immigration general information line is 0570-013904, on weekdays from 8:30 to 17:15, in multiple languages. It explains general rules only. It does not tell you how far your application has got or whether it will be approved.
- Benefit: The ISA's page says the telephone number of the Foreign Residents General Information Center (外国人在留総合インフォメーションセンター) is 「０５７０－０１３９０４」 ("0570-013904"), and the hours are 「午前８：３０～午後５：１５（土日・祝日・年末年始（12/29～1/3）を除く）」 ("8:30 a.m. to 5:15 p.m. (except Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays, and the year-end and New Year period (12/29 to 1/3))"). You can ask by telephone, at the counter or by email in several languages. The page lists what it can answer: general explanations of immigration and residence procedures, that is, content already on the official website. What it does not answer is the progress of an individual case and the prospects (見通し) of permission, and the page states that on these 「担当部署でも回答はできません」 ("the department in charge cannot answer either"). Calls are recorded.
- Evidence: A

- Notes: Details such as telephone numbers change. Go back to this page and check before you use it. The page warns that there are many cases of people misdialing and reaching a private home. If your individual case needs a written basis, go to a gyoseishoshi or an attorney.
