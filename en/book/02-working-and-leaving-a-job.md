# 2. Working in Japan and Leaving a Job

This section is about money. It covers what you should receive when you sign a contract, how overtime and annual leave are calculated, what protection the law gives when a contract ends or you are dismissed, and how to claim unemployment benefits after leaving a job.

Japanese labor law does not look at nationality. The same provisions apply to foreign nationals and Japanese nationals. This section uses the current text of the Labor Standards Act (労働基準法), the Labor Contracts Act (労働契約法), the Employment Insurance Act (雇用保険法) and the Civil Code (民法), as of 2026-10-05. What the law states is that "you have the right to ask". To actually get it, most of the time you first have to raise it yourself. If the company still does not give it, you go to the Labor Standards Inspection Office (労働基準監督署). Only if that fails do you go to court. This section did not verify how long each of these steps takes.

This section covers only people in an employment relationship. Whether these provisions apply to researchers who receive funding in the nature of a scholarship and have no labor contract with the institution, this section does not judge. Check the handbook of your own program.

Leaving a job also affects your residence status: you must notify immigration within 14 days, and your status may be revoked if you go more than 3 months without a job. See Section 1, Item 3 (notify immigration within 14 days each time you leave a job, start a job) and Section 1, Item 4 (do not stay without a job for more than 3 months after leaving).

### 1. Ask for written terms stating wages and working hours when you sign; if they differ from reality you can leave at once
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Ask once before you sign, and keep a copy for yourself once you have it.
- In plain words: When the company concludes a labor contract with you, it must state clearly the wages, working hours and other conditions, and items such as wages and working hours must be given to you in the prescribed manner. If after joining you find things differ from what you were told, you can cancel the contract at once. If you moved home for the job, the company pays your travel costs home if you return within 14 days.
- Benefit: Article 15, paragraph (1) of the Labor Standards Act says that the employer, when concluding a labor contract, 「賃金、労働時間その他の労働条件を明示しなければならない」 ("must clearly state the wages, working hours and other working conditions"). Among these, wages, working hours and other matters set by Order of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare must be stated in the manner set by that order. Paragraph (2) provides that where the stated conditions are in the situation 「事実と相違する場合」 ("where they differ from the actual facts"), the worker 「即時に労働契約を解除することができる」 ("may immediately cancel the labor contract"). Paragraph (3) provides that where a worker who changed residence for the job returns home within 14 days from the day of cancellation, the employer must bear the necessary travel expenses. For a violation of paragraph (1) or (3), Article 120 provides 「三十万円以下の罰金」 ("a fine of not more than 300,000 yen").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 労働基準法（昭和二十二年法律第四十九号）第十五条、第百二十条第一号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000049>
- Notes: Exactly which items must be stated, and whether they can be given by email, is written in the Enforcement Regulation. This section did not verify them item by item. Immigration also looks at this document when you apply for a status of residence, so it serves two purposes.

### 2. You do not have to accept a contract clause fixing "how much you pay if you quit early", and the company cannot deduct money it lent you from your wages
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. The price is reading the whole contract before you sign.
- In plain words: The company cannot write into the contract in advance "a penalty of so much if you do not work for so long". Money the company lent you on the condition that you come and work cannot be deducted directly from your wages. If the company does either of these, it can face a sentence of up to 6 months or a fine of up to 300,000 yen.
- Benefit: Article 16 of the Labor Standards Act provides that, with respect to non-performance of a labor contract, the employer 「違約金を定め、又は損害賠償額を予定する契約をしてはならない」 ("must not make a contract that fixes a penalty or sets in advance an amount of damages"). Article 17 provides that the employer must not set off against wages an advance or other claim for money lent in advance on the condition of work. For a violation of these two articles, Article 119, item (i) provides 「六月以下の拘禁刑又は三十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 6 months or a fine of not more than 300,000 yen").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 労働基準法 第十六条、第十七条、第百十九条第一号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000049>
- Notes: What Article 16 prohibits is fixing the amount in advance. If you really cause the company a loss and the company later claims its actual loss, that is outside what this article prohibits. Where the company pays for your training and you agree to repay it unless you work a set number of years, whether such an agreement violates Article 16 depends on the individual case. This section did not verify the case law.

### 3. Lower pay or extra shifts because of your nationality is illegal
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. If you want to pursue it, keep evidence that allows a comparison, such as the conditions of Japanese colleagues in the same position.
- In plain words: The company cannot treat you differently in wages, working hours or other working conditions because of your nationality. A violation can bring a sentence of up to 6 months or a fine of up to 300,000 yen.
- Benefit: Article 3 of the Labor Standards Act provides that the employer must not treat a worker differently in wages, working hours or other working conditions by reason of the worker's 「国籍、信条又は社会的身分」 ("nationality, creed or social status"). For a violation, Article 119, item (i) provides 「六月以下の拘禁刑又は三十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 6 months or a fine of not more than 300,000 yen").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 労働基準法 第三条、第百十九条第一号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000049>
- Notes: This article governs working conditions after you have joined. The provision does not say whether refusing to hire foreign nationals at the recruitment stage is covered. This section draws no conclusion. For where to take the matter in practice, see Item 12 (report it to the Labor Standards Inspection Office).

### 4. Overtime pay is at least 25% extra, 35% on days off, and 50% for the part over 60 hours a month
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Keep your own record of when you start and finish work each day.
- In plain words: Statutory working hours are 40 hours a week and 8 hours a day. Hours beyond that are paid at least 25% extra, and work on a statutory day off at least 35% extra. Work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. gets a further 25% extra. The part of overtime over 60 hours in a month is paid at least 50% extra. You can claim unpaid overtime pay for up to 3 years.
- Benefit: Article 32 of the Labor Standards Act provides that working hours, excluding rest periods, must not exceed 40 hours a week or 8 hours a day. To have people work overtime, the company must, under Article 36, conclude a written agreement with a representative of the majority of the workers and file a notification of it. The overtime limit in the agreement is 45 hours a month and 360 hours a year. Article 37, paragraph (1) provides that premium wages (割増賃金) must be paid for overtime and for work on days off, at rates set by Cabinet Order: 「二割五分」 ("25 percent") for extended working hours and 「三割五分」 ("35 percent") for work on days off. The proviso to the same paragraph provides that the part of extended hours over 60 hours in a month must be calculated at 「五割以上」 ("50 percent or more"). Paragraph (4) provides that work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. is calculated at 「二割五分以上」 ("25 percent or more"). Paragraph (5) provides that family allowance, commuting allowance and the like are not included in the base for the calculation. On the time limit for claiming wages, Article 115 says 5 years, and Article 143, paragraph (3) of the Supplementary Provisions provides that 「当分の間」 ("for the time being") it is 3 years, and 5 years for retirement allowances. Where a company does not pay overtime pay, a court can under Article 114 order it to pay a further additional payment (付加金) of the same amount, and the time limit for that claim is likewise 3 years for the time being. For a violation of Article 32 or Article 37, Article 119, item (i) provides 「六月以下の拘禁刑又は三十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 6 months or a fine of not more than 300,000 yen").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 労働基準法 第三十二条、第三十六条第一項・第四項・第十一項、第三十七条、第百十四条、第百十五条、第百十九条第一号、附則第百四十三条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000049>；労働基準法第三十七条第一項の時間外及び休日の割増賃金に係る率の最低限度を定める政令（平成六年政令第五号）. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/406CO0000000005>
- Notes: Article 36, paragraph (11) states that the limits of 45 hours a month and 360 hours a year do not apply to work on the research and development of new technologies, new products or new services. That the limits do not apply does not mean overtime pay need not be paid. Article 37 applies all the same. This section does not cover arrangements that are not counted by the hour, such as managers and supervisors (管理監督者) and the discretionary working system (裁量労働制). How "fixed overtime pay" written into a contract is calculated was also not verified.

### 5. After 6 months of work you get 10 days of paid annual leave, and the company must have you take 5 days each year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. You have to say yourself which days you want off.
- In plain words: Once you have worked continuously for 6 months from the day you joined and attended 80 percent or more of the working days, you get 10 days of paid annual leave. It increases each year after that, reaching 20 days at 6 and a half years. In principle you decide which days to take. For a person with 10 or more days of annual leave, the company must arrange for 5 of those days to be taken each year.
- Benefit: Article 39, paragraph (1) of the Labor Standards Act provides that a worker who has worked continuously for 6 months from the day of hiring and attended 80 percent or more of all working days is given 10 working days of paid leave. Paragraph (2) provides for added days with each further year: 1 more day at 1 and a half years, 2 more at 2 and a half years, 4 more at 3 and a half years, 6 more at 4 and a half years, 8 more at 5 and a half years, and 10 more at 6 and a half years or longer. Paragraph (5) provides that the employer must give the leave at the time the worker requests, and can move it to another time only where it would interfere with the normal operation of the business. Paragraph (7) provides that, for a worker with 10 or more working days of annual leave, the employer must designate the time for 5 of those days and have the worker take them within one year from the reference date. Paragraph (8) provides that days the worker has already taken on their own can be deducted from these 5 days. For a violation of Article 39 (except paragraph (7)), Article 119 provides 「六月以下の拘禁刑又は三十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 6 months or a fine of not more than 300,000 yen"). For a violation of paragraph (7), Article 120 provides 「三十万円以下の罰金」 ("a fine of not more than 300,000 yen").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 労働基準法 第三十九条第一項・第二項・第五項・第七項・第八項、第百十九条第一号、第百二十条第一号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000049>
- Notes: People who work fewer days a week are given leave in proportion. The numbers of days are in the Enforcement Regulation, and this section does not list them. This section did not verify against the original text until what year unused annual leave can be carried over, or whether the company must convert it into money when you leave.

### 6. Dismissal requires at least 30 days' notice, or 30 days' pay instead; a dismissal without proper grounds is void
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Do not sign any "voluntary resignation" document on the spot. First ask for the reasons for dismissal in writing.
- In plain words: If the company dismisses you, it must tell you at least 30 days in advance. If it does not, it must pay 30 days or more of average wages. This does not apply within the first 14 days of a probationary period. A dismissal that has no objectively reasonable grounds and is not accepted by society's general view is void in law. You can ask the company to issue a certificate stating the reasons for dismissal.
- Benefit: Article 20, paragraph (1) of the Labor Standards Act provides that notice of dismissal must be given at least 30 days in advance. An employer who gives no notice must pay 「三十日分以上の平均賃金」 ("average wages for 30 days or more"). Paragraph (2) provides that the number of days of notice can be reduced by the number of days for which average wages are paid. This does not apply where the business cannot continue because of a natural disaster or other unavoidable cause, or where the dismissal is for a reason attributable to the worker. Article 21 provides that Article 20 does not apply to a person in a probationary period, except one who has been employed continuously for more than 14 days. Article 16 of the Labor Contracts Act provides that a dismissal, 「客観的に合理的な理由を欠き、社会通念上相当であると認められない場合」 ("where it lacks objectively reasonable grounds and is not found to be appropriate in general societal terms"), is as an abuse of rights something the law 「無効とする」 ("treats as void"). Article 22, paragraph (2) of the Labor Standards Act provides that if, between the day of the notice and the day of leaving, the worker requests a certificate of the reasons for dismissal, the employer must deliver it 「遅滞なく」 ("without delay"). For a violation of Article 20, Article 119 provides 「六月以下の拘禁刑又は三十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 6 months or a fine of not more than 300,000 yen"), and a court can also order an additional payment of the same amount under Article 114.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 労働基準法 第二十条、第二十一条、第二十二条第二項、第百十四条、第百十九条第一号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000049>；労働契約法（平成十九年法律第百二十八号）第十六条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/419AC0000000128>
- Notes: "Void" has to be confirmed through a labor tribunal proceeding or a lawsuit. The provision does not automatically put you back at work. This section did not verify how long these steps take or how much they cost. In the provisions this section read, there is no severance payment calculated by years of service as there is in China. The money you can claim directly is these 30 days. Whether other laws provide one was not verified. Being dismissed and resigning yourself make a large difference for unemployment benefits; see Item 11 (claim unemployment benefits after leaving).

### 7. On a fixed-term contract the company cannot freely dismiss you before the term ends; if the contract is longer than 1 year, you can resign at any time after 1 year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain words: If you signed a contract with a fixed term and the term has not ended, the company cannot dismiss you without an unavoidable reason. If you yourself want to leave midway, in principle you also need an unavoidable reason. But if the contract term is longer than 1 year, once 1 year has passed from the start date you can resign at any time by saying so.
- Benefit: Article 17, paragraph (1) of the Labor Contracts Act provides that, for a fixed-term labor contract, the employer cannot dismiss the worker before the contract term expires 「やむを得ない事由がある場合でなければ」 ("unless there is an unavoidable reason"). Article 628 of the Civil Code provides that, where a term of employment has been set, either party can cancel immediately if there is an unavoidable reason; where the reason arises from one party's negligence, that party is liable to the other for damages. Article 137 of the Supplementary Provisions of the Labor Standards Act provides that a worker who has concluded a fixed-term labor contract with a term longer than 1 year is not bound by Article 628 of the Civil Code, and once 1 year has passed from the first day of the contract term, by notifying the employer the worker 「いつでも退職することができる」 ("may resign at any time"). This article does not apply to the persons in the items of Article 14, paragraph (1), that is, persons with highly specialized knowledge who can sign for up to 5 years and persons aged 60 or over. It also does not apply to contracts whose term is set for the completion of a specific project.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 労働契約法 第十七条第一項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/419AC0000000128>；民法（明治二十九年法律第八十九号）第六百二十八条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/129AC0000000089>；労働基準法 第十四条第一項、附則第百三十七条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000049>
- Notes: For a person whose contract term is 1 year or less and who wants to leave midway, the provision requires an "unavoidable reason". This section did not verify the case law on what counts as unavoidable. Ending the contract by agreement with the company is not restricted by this article. Article 137 of the Supplementary Provisions is written as a transitional measure, until the measures provided for in another law are put in place.

### 8. To resign from an indefinite-term contract, 2 weeks' notice is enough
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain words: If your employment has no fixed term, you can give notice of resignation at any time, and the contract ends 2 weeks after you give it. You do not need the company's consent.
- Benefit: Article 627, paragraph (1) of the Civil Code provides that, where the parties have not set a term of employment, either party 「いつでも解約の申入れをすることができる」 ("may give notice of termination at any time"), and the employment 「二週間を経過することによって終了する」 ("ends when two weeks have passed") from the day of the notice.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 民法 第六百二十七条第一項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/129AC0000000089>
- Notes: Company rules of employment (就業規則) often say "1 month in advance" or longer. This section did not verify against the original text which prevails when the rules of employment conflict with this article, and draws no conclusion. For the sake of your leaving certificate and the handover, follow the rules of employment where you can. For fixed-term contracts, see Item 7 (on a fixed-term contract before the term ends).

### 9. Fixed-term contracts totalling more than 5 years let you ask for conversion to indefinite-term; for researchers at universities and research institutions it is 10 years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. You must apply yourself before your current contract expires.
- In plain words: If your fixed-term contracts with the same employer add up to more than 5 years and you apply before the current contract expires, it automatically converts to an indefinite-term contract with the other conditions unchanged. For people doing research at universities and research and development agencies, this period is 10 years, and contracts signed while you were a student are not counted. If there is a gap of 6 months or more between two contracts, the years before it are reset to zero.
- Benefit: Article 18, paragraph (1) of the Labor Contracts Act provides that where a worker whose total contract term under two or more fixed-term labor contracts with the same employer is one that 「五年を超える」 ("exceeds five years") applies, on or before the day the current contract expires, to conclude an indefinite-term contract, 「使用者は当該申込みを承諾したものとみなす」 ("the employer is deemed to have accepted the application"). The working conditions are the same as in the current contract apart from the contract term, except for any part agreed otherwise. Paragraph (2) provides that where the gap between two contracts is 6 months or more, the contract terms before the gap are not counted in the total. Where the earlier contract was shorter than 1 year, the threshold for the gap is shorter. Article 15-2, paragraph (1) of the Act on the Promotion of Science, Technology and Innovation Creation (科学技術・イノベーション創出の活性化に関する法律) provides that, for researchers and the like who conclude a fixed-term labor contract with a research and development agency or with a person that establishes a university or similar institution, 「五年」 ("five years") is read as 「十年」 ("ten years"). Paragraph (2) of the same article provides that, for a fixed-term labor contract concluded while enrolled at a university, the period of enrollment is not counted in the total contract term. In addition, Article 14 of the Labor Standards Act provides that the maximum term of a single fixed-term contract is 3 years, and 5 years for persons with highly specialized knowledge and persons aged 60 or over.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 労働契約法 第十八条、第十九条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/419AC0000000128>；科学技術・イノベーション創出の活性化に関する法律（平成二十年法律第六十三号）第十五条の二. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/420AC0100000063>；労働基準法 第十四条第一項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000049>
- Notes: The conversion happens only when you apply. It does not happen automatically if you do not. Many institutions set the total contract length within 5 or 10 years, so ask when you join up to what year the contract can be renewed. When a contract expires and the company does not renew it, the company is also restricted in two situations: where the contract has been renewed repeatedly and non-renewal is in substance no different from dismissal; and where you have reasonable grounds to expect renewal. In these cases the company cannot refuse your application for renewal without objectively reasonable grounds (Article 19 of the Labor Contracts Act). Researchers without an employment relationship are not covered by this item.

### 10. Ask for two things when you leave: wages settled within 7 days, and a certificate of employment listing only the items you request
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Ask once in writing before you leave.
- In plain words: After you leave, if you ask, the company must pay your wages in full within 7 days and return money and goods that belong to you. You can ask for a certificate stating your period of employment, the work you did, your position, your wages and the reason for leaving. The company cannot write in items you did not ask for.
- Benefit: Article 23, paragraph (1) of the Labor Standards Act provides that when a worker dies or leaves employment and the person entitled makes a request, the employer must pay the wages 「七日以内」 ("within 7 days") and return reserve funds, deposits, savings and other money and goods to which the worker is entitled. Paragraph (2) provides that where there is a dispute, the undisputed part must be paid within that period. Article 22, paragraph (1) provides that when a worker leaving employment requests a certificate of the period of employment, the type of work, the position in the business, the wages or the reason for leaving, the employer must deliver it 「遅滞なく」 ("without delay"). Where the reason for leaving is dismissal, this includes the reasons for the dismissal. Paragraph (3) provides that the certificate must not include items the worker did not request. Paragraph (4) prohibits the employer from communicating, by prior arrangement with a third party and for the purpose of obstructing the worker's employment, about the worker's nationality, creed and the like, or from putting secret marks on the certificate. For a violation of Article 22, paragraphs (1) to (3) or of Article 23, Article 120 provides 「三十万円以下の罰金」 ("a fine of not more than 300,000 yen"). For a violation of Article 22, paragraph (4), Article 119 provides 「六月以下の拘禁刑又は三十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 6 months or a fine of not more than 300,000 yen").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 労働基準法 第二十二条、第二十三条、第百十九条第一号、第百二十条第一号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000049>
- Notes: The separation slip (離職票, rishoku-hyo) you need to claim unemployment benefits is a different document on the employment insurance side. It is not this certificate, and this section did not verify the deadline for issuing it. You must also notify immigration within 14 days of leaving; see Section 1, Item 3 (notify immigration within 14 days each time you leave a job, start a job).

### 11. After leaving, register as a job seeker at the Public Employment Security Office and claim unemployment benefits; if you resigned yourself, you wait 1 to 3 months longer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. You must register in person, then go for recognition once every 4 weeks, and keep a record of your job-seeking activities.
- In plain words: A person who paid into employment insurance for 12 months or more in the 2 years before leaving can receive the basic allowance (基本手当) when unemployed. For people who were dismissed, or whose contract expired and was not renewed although they wanted renewal, 6 months in the 1 year before leaving is enough. After registering, you first wait 7 days. If you resigned yourself without a justifiable reason, you wait a further 1 to 3 months. An ordinary recipient can receive 90 to 150 days, and must finish receiving them within 1 year of leaving.
- Benefit: Article 13, paragraph (1) of the Employment Insurance Act provides that the basic allowance is paid to a person whose periods as an insured person in the 2 years before the day of separation total 「十二箇月以上」 ("twelve months or more"). Paragraph (2) provides that for persons separated for specified reasons (特定理由離職者) and specified qualified recipients (特定受給資格者), this becomes a total of 「六箇月」 ("six months") or more in the 1 year before the day of separation. Paragraph (3) defines a person separated for specified reasons as a person whose fixed-term labor contract expired and was not renewed, where the person wished for renewal but no agreement was reached, and other persons who left for unavoidable reasons. Article 23, paragraph (2) defines a specified qualified recipient as a person who left because of bankruptcy or the reduction or abolition of the business, and a person who was dismissed or the like, except one dismissed for a serious reason attributable to that person. Article 15, paragraph (2) requires the person to register as a job seeker at the Public Employment Security Office (公共職業安定所, also known as Hello Work) after separation, and paragraph (3) provides that recognition of unemployment takes place once every 4 weeks. Article 21 provides that no payment is made for the period until the days of unemployment after the job-seeker registration total 7 days. Article 33, paragraph (1) provides that in the case of dismissal for a serious reason attributable to the person, or 「正当な理由がなく自己の都合によつて退職した場合」 ("where the person has left for personal reasons without a justifiable reason"), no payment is made, after the waiting period ends, for a period set by the head of the Public Employment Security Office that is 「一箇月以上三箇月以内」 ("not less than one month and not more than three months"). Persons who received prescribed education or training within one year and the like are excepted. Article 22, paragraph (1) sets the prescribed number of days of benefits: 90 days where the basic period for calculation is under 10 years, 120 days for 10 years or more and under 20 years, and 150 days for 20 years or more. Article 20, paragraph (1) provides that the payment period is in principle 1 year from the day after the day of separation.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 雇用保険法（昭和四十九年法律第百十六号）第六条、第十三条、第十五条、第二十条、第二十一条、第二十二条、第二十三条、第三十三条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/349AC0000000116>
- Notes: A person whose prescribed working hours are under 20 hours a week, and a person expected to be employed for fewer than 31 days, is not an insured person. Students are also excluded under ministerial order (Article 6). So a student's part-time job generally does not come with this insurance. This section did not verify against the current explanation of the Public Employment Security Office how much is paid per day, how many months a person who resigned actually waits, or how many days a specified qualified recipient can receive, so it gives no figures. What happens to a foreign national's status of residence while receiving unemployment benefits is not in the provisions. Read this together with Section 1, Item 4 (do not stay without a job for more than 3 months after leaving) and ask immigration.

### 12. If the company breaks the law, you can report it to the Labor Standards Inspection Office, and the company cannot retaliate
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Bring evidence: the contract, pay slips, and your own record of your working hours.
- In plain words: Not paying overtime pay, not giving annual leave, dismissing without notice: all of these violate the Labor Standards Act, and you can report them to the Labor Standards Inspection Office. The company cannot dismiss you or treat you unfavorably because you reported. If it does, it can face a sentence of up to 6 months or a fine of up to 300,000 yen.
- Benefit: Article 104, paragraph (1) of the Labor Standards Act provides that where there is, at a workplace, a fact that violates this Act or an order issued under this Act, the worker 「その事実を行政官庁又は労働基準監督官に申告することができる」 ("may report that fact to the administrative office or to a labor standards inspector"). Paragraph (2) provides that the employer, by reason of the report, 「解雇その他不利益な取扱をしてはならない」 ("must not dismiss the worker or otherwise treat the worker disadvantageously"). For a violation of paragraph (2), Article 119, item (i) provides 「六月以下の拘禁刑又は三十万円以下の罰金」 ("imprisonment for not more than 6 months or a fine of not more than 300,000 yen").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 労働基準法 第百四条、第百十九条第一号. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000049>
- Notes: The Inspection Office deals with violations of the Labor Standards Act. Whether a dismissal has proper grounds, and whether a contract should be renewed, are civil disputes under the Labor Contracts Act, and it does not decide them.
