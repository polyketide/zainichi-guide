# 5. Renting

This section counts money. It covers which charges are capped when you sign, whether the landlord can make you leave or raise the rent at will while you live there, and how much of the deposit should come back when you move out.

Japanese law clearly leans toward the person living in the home when it comes to renting. But most of the rights written in the law only take effect if you raise them yourself. If you raise them and the other side refuses, the next step is conciliation (調停) or a lawsuit. This section did not verify how long those steps take or what they cost.

The provisions come from the current text of the Civil Code (民法), the Act on Land and Building Leases (借地借家法), the Real Estate Brokerage Act (宅地建物取引業法) and the Consumer Contract Act (消費者契約法), plus the public notice and guidelines of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (国土交通省), as of 2026-10-05.

Refusal to rent to foreigners, screening by guarantee companies, key money (礼金) and renewal fees (更新料): for these, no statutory text was found that could be checked sentence by sentence, so this section does not cover them.

### 1. The legal cap on the agent's fee is one month's rent; without your prior consent, the agent can take at most half a month from you
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Before you sign the brokerage request, ask one question: "How much is the fee, and what is it based on?"
- In plain words: The agent's fee for a rental cannot exceed one month's rent, counting what the landlord and the tenant pay together. For a residential lease, if the agent did not get your consent when accepting your request, the agent can take at most half a month from you. Charging above the cap is illegal.
- Benefit: Article 46, paragraph 1 of the Real Estate Brokerage Act (宅地建物取引業法) provides that the amount of the agent's remuneration is set by the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism. Paragraph 2 provides that a real estate broker (宅地建物取引業者) 「前項の額をこえて報酬を受けてはならない」 ("must not receive remuneration exceeding the amount in the preceding paragraph"). The public notice (告示) provides that for brokerage of a lease, the total remuneration received from both clients (依頼者) is 「一月分に相当する金額以内」 ("within an amount equivalent to one month") of the rent. The same clause continues: for brokerage of a lease of a residential building, the remuneration received from one client is 「当該媒介の依頼を受けるに当たって当該依頼者の承諾を得ている場合を除き、借賃の一月分の二分の一に相当する金額以内とする」 ("within an amount equivalent to one half of one month's rent, except where that client's consent was obtained when accepting the request for that brokerage"). The notice also provides that no remuneration may be received beyond these amounts; the cost of advertising that the client specifically asked for and the amount equivalent to consumption tax are excepted.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 宅地建物取引業法（昭和二十七年法律第百七十六号）第四十六条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/327AC1000000176>；建設省. 宅地建物取引業者が宅地又は建物の売買等に関して受けることができる報酬の額（昭和四十五年建設省告示第千五百五十二号）. 国土交通省 <https://www.mlit.go.jp/notice/noticedata/sgml/1970/26197000/26197000.html>
- Notes: This page of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism carries the old text as amended up to Heisei 9. The notice has been amended again since, and this section did not obtain the full current text. Whether the one month and the half month in the lease brokerage clause have changed must be checked against the current notice; consumption tax is added on top. In practice the tenant commonly pays the full month. Under the wording of the notice, that requires that the agent got your consent when accepting the request. This sentence is a commonly repeated account; this section has no statistics for it and did not verify what form the consent must take. Whether items such as a "cleaning fee", "disinfection fee" or "24-hour support fee" are remuneration in disguise was not verified in this section.

### 2. Before you sign, the agent must explain the important matters face to face, and the person explaining must show a qualification certificate
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Spend half an hour to an hour listening to the end, and ask on the spot about anything you do not understand.
- In plain words: Before the contract is concluded, the agent must have a qualified person explain the important matters of the home and the contract to you, using written material. The person explaining must show a real estate transaction agent certificate (宅地建物取引士証), and the material must carry that person's name. If you are asked to sign without a clear explanation, the agent is breaking the law.
- Benefit: Article 35, paragraph 1 of the Real Estate Brokerage Act provides that a real estate broker must, before the contract is concluded, have a licensed real estate transaction agent (宅地建物取引士) deliver a document on the statutory matters to the person who is going to rent and explain it. Paragraph 4 provides that when explaining, the transaction agent 「宅地建物取引士証を提示しなければならない」 ("must present the real estate transaction agent certificate"). Paragraph 5 provides that the transaction agent's name must be stated on the document delivered.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 宅地建物取引業法 第三十五条第一項・第四項・第五項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/327AC1000000176>
- Notes: The specific matters to be explained for a lease are listed at length in the items of the article and in the enforcement regulations; this section does not list them one by one. Whether the explanation can be given online, and whether an electronic file can replace the written document, was not verified in this section. The explanation is given in Japanese, and there is no statutory duty to translate. If you cannot follow it, bring someone who can, or take the material home and read it through before signing.

### 3. If an individual acts as your joint guarantor, the contract must state a maximum guaranteed amount; a guarantee without one is void
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain words: If a relative or friend guarantees your rent as an individual, the contract must state the most that person can be made to pay. Without that cap, the guarantee does not take effect. The other way round, when someone asks you to be their rental guarantor, look at this figure first. It is the most you would have to pay for them.
- Benefit: Article 465-2, paragraph 1 of the Civil Code provides that under a revolving guarantee contract (根保証契約) where the guarantor is not a corporation, the guarantor is liable to perform 「極度額を限度として」 ("up to the maximum amount"), covering everything: the principal of the main obligation, interest, penalties, damages and so on. Paragraph 2 provides: 「個人根保証契約は、前項に規定する極度額を定めなければ、その効力を生じない。」 ("A personal revolving guarantee contract does not take effect unless it sets the maximum amount provided for in the preceding paragraph.")
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 民法（明治二十九年法律第八十九号）第四百六十五条の二. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/129AC0000000089>
- Notes: This provision governs individual guarantors. A guarantee company is a corporation, so the provision does not apply to it. Foreigners are often required to use a guarantee company when renting. Whether there are legal rules on a guarantee company's guarantee fee, screening standards or collection methods was not verified against the original text in this section.

### 4. When an ordinary lease expires, the landlord needs just cause to refuse renewal and must give notice 6 months to 1 year ahead
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain words: When an ordinary lease reaches its end and the landlord has not given notice of non-renewal between 1 year and 6 months before the end, the contract renews automatically on the same terms. A landlord's notice of non-renewal or request to terminate must have just cause. If the landlord requests termination, the lease ends only after 6 months. Contract clauses that are worse for you than this are void.
- Benefit: Article 26, paragraph 1 of the Act on Land and Building Leases (借地借家法) provides that where a building lease has a fixed term and neither party notifies the other of non-renewal 「一年前から六月前までの間」 ("between one year and six months before") the end of the term, the contract is deemed renewed on the same terms as the original contract, and the term after renewal is treated as not fixed. Article 27, paragraph 1 provides that where the lessor requests termination, the lease 「六月を経過することによって終了する」 ("ends when six months have passed") from the day of the request. Article 28 provides that the lessor's notice of non-renewal or request to terminate must take into account each party's need to use the building, the history of the lease, the use and present state of the building, and any property benefit offered by the lessor, and 「正当の事由があると認められる場合でなければ、することができない」 ("may not be made unless there is found to be just cause"). Article 30: 「この節の規定に反する特約で建物の賃借人に不利なものは、無効とする。」 ("Any special provision that is contrary to the provisions of this Section and is disadvantageous to the building lessee is void.")
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 借地借家法（平成三年法律第九十号）第二十六条、第二十七条、第二十八条、第三十条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/403AC0000000090>
- Notes: What counts as just cause is decided by the courts case by case; this section did not verify case law. The "property benefit" in the provision is what is usually called a relocation payment. There is no statutory standard for whether it is paid or how much. If the contract is cancelled because you owe rent or breach the contract, that is a different matter and this provision does not protect you. This item does not apply to Item 5 (fixed-term lease contracts).

### 5. If you sign a "fixed-term lease" (定期借家), you must move out when it expires; check the contract type before signing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Before signing, look for the words 「定期建物賃貸借」 ("fixed-term building lease").
- In plain words: A fixed-term lease ends when the term is up and does not renew. It must be signed in writing, and the landlord must also give you a separate document beforehand explaining that "this contract does not renew and ends at the end of the term". Without that explanation, the clause is void. For a contract of one year or longer, the landlord must notify you between 1 year and 6 months before the end. If the home is under 200 square metres and you can no longer live there because of an unavoidable event such as a job transfer, medical treatment or caring for a relative, you can terminate with 1 month's notice.
- Benefit: Article 38, paragraph 1 of the Act on Land and Building Leases provides that a building lease with a fixed term may stipulate that the contract will not be renewed only where it is concluded in writing, such as by notarial deed; this is the fixed-term building lease (定期建物賃貸借). Paragraph 3 provides that the lessor must deliver a document to the lessee in advance and explain that the lease 「契約の更新がなく、期間の満了により当該建物の賃貸借は終了する」 ("will not be renewed, and the lease of the building will end when the term expires"). Paragraph 5 provides that where the lessor has not given this explanation, the stipulation of non-renewal 「無効とする」 ("is void"). Paragraph 6 provides that where the term is one year or longer, the lessor must give notice that the lease will end on expiry between one year and six months before the end of the term; otherwise the lessor cannot assert the ending against the lessee. Paragraph 7 provides that for a residential building with a floor area under two hundred square metres, where 「転勤、療養、親族の介護その他のやむを得ない事情」 ("a job transfer, medical treatment, caring for a relative, or other unavoidable circumstances") make it difficult to use the building as the base of the lessee's life, the lessee may request termination, and the lease 「一月を経過することによって終了する」 ("ends when one month has passed") from the day of the request. Paragraph 8 provides that special provisions contrary to paragraphs 6 and 7 and disadvantageous to the lessee are void.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 借地借家法 第三十八条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/403AC0000000090>
- Notes: If you want to stay after the term ends, the only way is to sign a new contract with the landlord. The landlord may decline, or may change the terms. The contract and the explanatory document may be provided electronically; the conditions are in paragraphs 2 and 4. If your job has a fixed term or you are not sure how long you will stay, compare the lease end date with your period of stay and your employment contract period.

### 6. If the landlord wants to raise the rent and you disagree, you can pay the amount you think is reasonable until a court decides
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Holding out may mean going through conciliation or a lawsuit. If you lose, you pay the shortfall plus interest at ten percent a year.
- In plain words: When the rent has become unsuitable because of changes in tax burden, property prices or nearby rents, the landlord can ask for an increase, and you can ask for a reduction. If the landlord wants an increase and you cannot agree, it is enough to pay the amount you think is reasonable until a court judgment becomes final. If the judgment fixes a higher amount, you pay the shortfall plus interest at ten percent a year.
- Benefit: Article 32, paragraph 1 of the Act on Land and Building Leases provides that when the building rent has become unreasonable because of changes in economic circumstances, such as an increase or decrease in taxes and other burdens or a rise or fall in the price of the land or building, or in comparison with the rent of similar buildings nearby, the parties may request an increase or reduction of the rent for the future regardless of the contract terms; where there is a special provision that the rent will not be increased for a certain period, that provision applies. Paragraph 2 provides that where no agreement is reached on an increase, the person who received the request, until a judgment finding the increase justified becomes final, 「相当と認める額の建物の借賃を支払うことをもって足りる」 ("need only pay building rent in the amount that person finds reasonable"); once the judgment is final, if the amount already paid falls short, that person must pay the shortfall plus 「年一割の割合による支払期後の利息」 ("interest after the due date at a rate of ten percent per year").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 借地借家法 第三十二条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/403AC0000000090>
- Notes: Paying nothing, or paying an obviously low amount, may be treated as unpaid rent, which is a separate route to having the contract cancelled. So pay at least the original amount. How long conciliation and litigation take and what they cost was not verified in this section. This provision does not apply to a fixed-term lease that contains a special provision on rent revision (Article 38, paragraph 9).

### 7. The landlord repairs what breaks; for the part that becomes unusable, the rent goes down accordingly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. On the day you find a problem, notify the landlord or the management company in a way that leaves a record, and take photos.
- In plain words: Repairs needed for using the home are the landlord's duty, except for what you broke yourself. If part of the home becomes unusable for a reason that is not your fault, such as a leaking roof or a broken water heater, the rent is reduced in proportion to the unusable part. If what remains cannot serve the purpose of the lease, you can cancel the contract.
- Benefit: Article 606, paragraph 1 of the Civil Code provides that the lessor has a duty to make the repairs necessary for the use and profit of the leased property, except where the repair is needed because of something the lessee is responsible for. Article 611, paragraph 1 provides that where part of the leased property can no longer be used and profited from because of loss or another cause, and this is not due to something the lessee is responsible for, 「賃料は、その使用及び収益をすることができなくなった部分の割合に応じて、減額される」 ("the rent is reduced in proportion to the part that can no longer be used and profited from"). Paragraph 2 provides that where the remaining part alone cannot achieve the purpose of the lease, the lessee may cancel the contract.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 民法 第六百六条、第六百十一条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/129AC0000000089>
- Notes: The provision says the rent "is reduced", but there is no statutory table for how much, so you have to negotiate with the landlord. Whether you can do the repair yourself and then claim the cost when the landlord keeps delaying is in Article 607-2, which this section did not read. Do not cut your rent payment on your own; the reason is the same as in Item 6 (the landlord wants to raise the rent and you disagree).

### 8. For a lease with no fixed term, give 3 months' notice when you want to move out; for a lease with a fixed term, check the contract
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain words: For a home lease with no fixed term, either side can request termination at any time, and the lease ends 3 months after the request. For a contract with a fixed term, leaving midway depends on the termination clause in the contract. Notice of 1 or 2 months is common.
- Benefit: Article 617, paragraph 1 of the Civil Code provides that where the parties have not fixed a term for the lease, either party may request termination at any time, and a lease of a building ends when 「三箇月」 ("three months") have passed from the day of the request.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 民法 第六百十七条第一項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/129AC0000000089>
- Notes: "Notice of 1 or 2 months" is common contract wording, not a statutory provision. Go by your own contract. A contract that renews automatically after its term becomes a lease with no fixed term under Item 4 (when an ordinary lease expires). When the landlord requests termination, the period is 6 months and just cause is also required, so the two sides are not symmetrical. For whether there is a cap on clauses that charge a penalty for moving out within a short period, see Item 10 (cancellation penalties and late charges).

### 9. When you move out, you do not pay for wear from normal use or for aging; the deposit must be returned after what you owe is deducted
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Photograph the whole home on the day you move in and again on the day you move out, and keep the photos with their dates.
- In plain words: What you must restore when you move out is the damage caused after you moved in. Wear from normal use and aging over time do not count. After the contract ends and the home is handed back, the deposit must be returned, less unpaid rent and what you are liable to pay. The guidelines of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism state that restoration to original condition does not mean returning the home to how it was when first rented.
- Benefit: Article 621 of the Civil Code provides that the lessee has a duty of restoration to original condition (原状回復) when the lease ends, for damage arising after receiving the leased property; the parenthesis states that damage does not include 「通常の使用及び収益によって生じた賃借物の損耗並びに賃借物の経年変化」 ("wear of the leased property caused by ordinary use and profit, and aging of the leased property"). Damage that is not due to something the lessee is responsible for is also excluded. Article 622-2, paragraph 1 provides that a lessor who has received a security deposit (敷金) must, once the lease has ended and the leased property has been returned, deduct the lessee's monetary obligations under the lease from the deposit and 「残額を返還しなければならない」 ("must return the remaining amount"); whatever it is called, any money handed over to secure these obligations counts as a security deposit. The guidelines of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism define restoration to original condition as restoring wear and damage caused by the lessee's intent, negligence, breach of the duty of care of a good manager, or other use beyond ordinary use, with the cost borne by the lessee; the cost of repairing aging and wear from ordinary use is 「賃料に含まれるもの」 ("included in the rent"). The guidelines' own words are: 「原状回復は、賃借人が借りた当時の状態に戻すことではないことを明確化」 ("clarifies that restoration to original condition does not mean returning the property to the state it was in when the lessee rented it").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 民法 第六百二十一条、第六百二十二条の二. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/129AC0000000089>；国土交通省. 「原状回復をめぐるトラブルとガイドライン」について. <https://www.mlit.go.jp/jutakukentiku/house/jutakukentiku_house_tk3_000020.html>
- Notes: The guidelines themselves state that they are a reference for the time of signing. A contract already signed is in principle handled according to the contract, and the guidelines are used in negotiation only where the contract is unclear. Where the contract adds a special provision such as "the tenant bears the move-out cleaning fee", whether it is valid depends on the case; this section did not verify case law. Which kind of damage falls on whom, and over how many years wallpaper and flooring depreciate, is in the tables in the body of the guidelines. This section read only the overview, not the full text. The Civil Code does not state a number of days within which the deposit must be returned.

### 10. Cancellation penalties and late charges in the contract are capped: the part above average damage and the part above 14.6% a year are void
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Before signing, find the two clauses on penalties and on late-payment damages and take a look.
- In plain words: A contract you sign as an individual with a landlord or management company that rents out homes as a business is a consumer contract. The part of a cancellation penalty that exceeds the other side's average damage when contracts of the same kind are cancelled is void. The part of late-rent damages that exceeds 14.6% a year is void. Clauses that one-sidedly harm the consumer's interests and breach the principle of good faith are also void.
- Benefit: Article 9, paragraph 1, item 1 of the Consumer Contract Act (消費者契約法) provides that for clauses that fix in advance the damages or penalty on cancellation, where the total exceeds, on cancellation of a consumer contract of the same kind, 「当該事業者に生ずべき平均的な損害の額を超えるもの」 ("the amount of average damage that would be caused to that business operator"), the excess is void. Item 2 provides that for clauses that fix in advance the damages or penalty for late payment, where the total exceeds the amount calculated by multiplying the unpaid amount by 「年十四・六パーセント」 ("14.6 percent per year"), the excess is void. Article 10 provides that clauses which restrict the consumer's rights or increase the consumer's duties compared with the application of non-mandatory provisions of laws and regulations, and which, contrary to the basic principle in Article 1, paragraph 2 of the Civil Code, 「消費者の利益を一方的に害するものは、無効とする」 ("unilaterally harm the interests of the consumer, are void").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 消費者契約法（平成十二年法律第六十一号）第九条第一項、第十条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/412AC0000000061>
- Notes: The provision gives no figure for "average damage"; it has to be settled by negotiation or by a court. This section did not verify case law. Where the landlord is an individual who does not rent out homes as a business, whether the contract counts as a consumer contract depends on whether the landlord is a "business operator" (事業者). This section draws no conclusion. Clauses on renewal fees, key money and non-refundable deductions from the deposit (敷引) are often disputed under Article 10, and results vary from case to case. This section did not verify case law and states no conclusion.
