# 4. Obligations in both countries: you are in Japan, so what remains on the China side

This section counts money and personal liberty. When you move to Japan, your tax, foreign exchange, social insurance, nationality and documents on the China side do not stop with the move. Each side has its own set of rules. Neither makes allowances for the other. You have to line up the things caught in between by yourself.

This section uses the original text of Chinese laws and departmental rules, taken from the National Immigration Administration (国家移民管理局), the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (国家外汇管理局), the State Taxation Administration (国家税务总局) and the Chinese government portal (中国政府网), as of 2026-10-05. The corresponding rules on the Japan side are written in the first three sections. Here they are only pointed to.

One thing has to be made clear first: for tax purposes, each country decides under its own law which country you are a resident of, and both may say you are theirs. This section only lays out the provisions. It draws no conclusion for anyone. For an individual case, ask a tax accountant or the tax authorities of the two countries.

Passport renewal, expiry of the resident identity card, and household registration (户口) are not written in this section, because the original text was not obtained.

### 1. First work out whether you count as a tax resident in China: you have a domicile there, or you stay a full 183 days in a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Count how many days you spent in China each year, and think through on which side your home and your economic centre lie.
- In plain words: A person who has a domicile in China, or who has no domicile but stays in China for a full 183 days in a year, is a resident individual (居民个人) of China, and pays individual income tax in China on income from both inside and outside China. "Having a domicile" does not look at whether you are physically there. It looks at whether household registration, family and economic interests make you habitually reside in China. When both countries treat you as a resident, the treaty says the competent tax authorities of the two countries settle it by consultation.
- Benefit: Article 1 of the Individual Income Tax Law (个人所得税法): 「在中国境内有住所，或者无住所而一个纳税年度内在中国境内居住累计满一百八十三天的个人，为居民个人。居民个人从中国境内和境外取得的所得，依照本法规定缴纳个人所得税。」 ("An individual who has a domicile in China, or who has no domicile but has resided in China for a cumulative total of 183 days or more in a tax year, is a resident individual. A resident individual pays individual income tax under this Law on income derived from inside and outside China.") Anyone not in these two groups is a non-resident individual (非居民个人) and pays tax only on income derived from inside China. The tax year runs from January 1 to December 31 of the Gregorian calendar. Article 2 of the Implementation Regulations (个人所得税法实施条例): having a domicile in China 「是指因户籍、家庭、经济利益关系而在中国境内习惯性居住」 ("means habitually residing in China because of household registration, family, or economic interests"). Article 4, paragraph 1 of the China-Japan tax treaty (中日税收协定) defines a "resident of a Contracting State" (「缔约国一方居民」) as a person who, under the laws of that State, is liable to tax there by reason of domicile, residence or similar criteria. Paragraph 2 provides that for an individual who is a resident of both Contracting States, 「缔约国双方主管当局应当通过协议，确定该人为本协定中缔约国一方的居民」 ("the competent authorities of the Contracting States shall determine by mutual agreement the Contracting State of which that person is a resident for the purposes of this Agreement").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018 年修正). 个人所得税法（第一条）. <https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5193028/content.html>；国务院 (2018). 个人所得税法实施条例（国务院令第 707 号，第二条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/22/content_5351177.htm>；中华人民共和国政府和日本国政府关于对所得避免双重征税和防止偷漏税的协定（第四条）. 国家税务总局广东省税务局刊登 <https://guangdong.chinatax.gov.cn/gdsw/gzsw_zcq_zczy_tysc/202109/0d4ed3c308494586b33de061dc7c4e55/files/c2c7d6d5e3ed44ff80d8f41680430dd4.pdf>
- Notes: Whether a person who lives long-term in Japan with household registration still in China counts as "habitually residing" (「习惯性居住」) is not answered by the provisions with a number of days or a list. This section did not verify the original text of any State Taxation Administration interpretation, and draws no conclusion. This paragraph of the China-Japan treaty gives no item-by-item order of tests. It says the authorities of the two countries consult. For how the Japan side decides, see Section 3, Item 8 (after 5 years of living in Japan, foreign income is also taxed).

### 2. If you count as a resident individual of China, report your Japanese income to China between March and June of the following year; tax paid in Japan can be credited
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Once a year, and you need to prepare Japanese proof of tax payment. Whether you still owe more tax on the China side after the credit depends on the difference between the two tax rates.
- In plain words: A resident individual of China who derives income from outside China must report it between March 1 and June 30 of the year after the income was derived. Income tax already paid in Japan can be credited, but not beyond the tax computed on that income under Chinese tax law. The credit requires a tax payment certificate issued by Japan. If you cannot produce one, no credit is given.
- Benefit: Article 13, paragraph 3 of the Individual Income Tax Law: 「居民个人从中国境外取得所得的，应当在取得所得的次年三月一日至六月三十日内申报纳税。」 ("A resident individual who derives income from outside China shall file a tax return and pay tax between March 1 and June 30 of the year following the year in which the income is derived.") Article 7 provides that for income a resident individual derives from outside China, the individual income tax already paid abroad may be credited against the tax payable, 「但抵免额不得超过该纳税人境外所得依照本法规定计算的应纳税额」 ("but the credit shall not exceed the tax payable on that taxpayer's foreign income as computed under this Law"). Article 8 of Announcement No. 3 of 2020 of the Ministry of Finance and the State Taxation Administration sets the place of filing: file with the competent tax authority where your employer in China is located; if you have no employer in China, file with the competent tax authority of your place of household registration or your habitual residence in China. Article 10 provides that when claiming the credit you must provide tax payment evidence such as a tax clearance certificate, tax payment receipt or tax payment record issued by the foreign taxing body, and 「未提供符合要求的纳税凭证，不予抵免」 ("where tax payment evidence meeting the requirements is not provided, no credit is given"). Article 13 provides that failure to file as required is handled under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection (税收征收管理法) and other rules, 「并按规定纳入个人纳税信用管理」 ("and is entered into individual tax credit management as provided"). Article 23 of the China-Japan tax treaty provides that for income a resident of China derives from Japan, the amount of Japanese tax paid under the treaty shall be allowed as a credit against Chinese tax, and the credit shall not exceed the corresponding amount of tax computed under Chinese tax law.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018 年修正). 个人所得税法（第七条、第十三条）. <https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5193028/content.html>；财政部、税务总局 (2020). 关于境外所得有关个人所得税政策的公告（2020 年第 3 号，第七、八、十、十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2020-01/22/content_5471604.htm>；中日税收协定（第二十三条）. <https://guangdong.chinatax.gov.cn/gdsw/gzsw_zcq_zczy_tysc/202109/0d4ed3c308494586b33de061dc7c4e55/files/c2c7d6d5e3ed44ff80d8f41680430dd4.pdf>
- Notes: This item assumes the answer to Item 1 (first work out whether you count as a tax resident in China) is "yes". If you are not a resident individual of China, you pay tax only on income from inside China, such as rent and interest in China. This section did not verify official figures on what the penalty for not filing is, or whether it is actually checked. This section did not verify which certificate from the Japan side counts as "tax payment evidence" (「纳税凭证」).

### 3. The treaty has one exemption for your first three years teaching or doing research at a Japanese university, and one for students studying in Japan
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Fill in one 「租税条約に関する届出書」 ("Application Form for Income Tax Convention") and give it to the institution that pays your remuneration, which passes it on to the tax office.
- In plain words: The China-Japan tax treaty says: if a person who was previously a resident of China stays temporarily in Japan to teach or do research at a university or other educational institution, and the stay is no more than three years from first arrival, Japan exempts that remuneration from tax. Payments a student receives for maintenance and education are also exempt in Japan. To use the exemption, you must submit the form to the tax office through the institution that pays you, by the day before you first receive remuneration. This is what the treaty and the procedure say. Whether it applies to you depends on the specific conditions.
- Benefit: Article 20 of the China-Japan tax treaty: any individual who is, or immediately before visiting a Contracting State was, a resident of the other Contracting State, and who stays temporarily mainly for the purpose of teaching, lecturing or research at a university, college, school or other recognised educational institution in the first-mentioned Contracting State, 「从其第一次到达之日起停留时间不超过三年的，该缔约国一方应对其由于教学、讲学或研究取得的报酬，免予征税」 ("where the stay does not exceed three years from the date of first arrival, that Contracting State shall exempt from tax the remuneration derived from such teaching, lecturing or research"). Article 21: where a student, business apprentice or trainee who is, or immediately before visiting a Contracting State was, a resident of the other Contracting State stays in the first-mentioned Contracting State solely for the purpose of education, training or obtaining special technical experience, 「其为了维持生活、接受教育或培训的目的收到的款项或所得，该缔约国一方应免予征税」 ("payments or income received for the purpose of maintenance, education or training shall be exempt from tax in that Contracting State"). The procedure page of Japan's National Tax Agency (国税庁) states: a person engaged in education or research at a school provided for in Article 1 of the School Education Act (elementary school, junior high school, high school, university, college of technology and so on) who is to be exempted from withholding on that remuneration under a tax treaty must file the 「租税条約に関する届出」 ("notification concerning a tax treaty"). The time for filing is 「入国の日以後最初に報酬・交付金等の支払を受ける日の前日まで」 ("by the day before the day you first receive payment of remuneration, grants, etc. after the date of entry"), and you must file in the same way when the items stated change. The method is to prepare one form for each payer and give it to the payer, who submits it to its competent tax office. The National Tax Agency leaflet for foreign nationals states: if tax was withheld because you did not file the form, you can submit the form together with the 「租税条約に関する源泉徴収税額の還付請求書（様式11）」 ("Application Form for Refund of the Overpaid Withholding Tax under the Income Tax Convention (Form 11)") to the tax office through your employer and get back the withheld income tax.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国政府和日本国政府关于对所得避免双重征税和防止偷漏税的协定（第二十条、第二十一条）. 国家税务总局广东省税务局刊登 <https://guangdong.chinatax.gov.cn/gdsw/gzsw_zcq_zczy_tysc/202109/0d4ed3c308494586b33de061dc7c4e55/files/c2c7d6d5e3ed44ff80d8f41680430dd4.pdf>；国税庁. A3-11 租税条約に関する届出（教授等・留学生・事業等の修習者・交付金等の受領者の報酬・交付金等に対する所得税及び復興特別所得税の免除）. <https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/tetsuzuki/shinsei/annai/joyaku/annai/1648_46.htm>；国税庁 (令和 7 年 10 月). 日本における給与に係る源泉徴収制度の概要（外国人向けリーフレット）. <https://www.nta.go.jp/publication/pamph/gensen/0022011-083.pdf>；国税庁. 質疑応答事例 入国後、2年を超えて滞在することとなった場合の交換教授免税（日伊租税条約）. <https://www.nta.go.jp/law/shitsugi/gensen/06/17.htm>
- Notes: The treaty says 「从其第一次到达之日起停留时间不超过三年」 ("the stay does not exceed three years from the date of first arrival"). For a person who first studied in Japan for some years and then stayed on to do research, this section did not find a National Tax Agency explanation specific to the China-Japan treaty on which day the three years run from or whether any of them remain. This section draws no conclusion. In that situation, first ask the administrative office of the paying institution or the tax office. This section also did not find an explanation specific to the China-Japan treaty on how the tax exempted earlier is handled once the stay exceeds three years. The National Tax Agency has a case on the Japan-Italy treaty: where the stay was originally planned for 2 years or less and was later extended beyond 2 years, the exemption applies up to "the day it became clear that it would exceed", and remuneration after that is taxed. The wording of the Japan-Italy treaty differs from the China-Japan treaty. This case can serve only as a reference and cannot be applied directly. The form deals with the withholding step, and what is exempted is income tax and the special income tax for reconstruction (復興特別所得税). This section did not verify how resident tax (住民税) is handled. For a researcher with no employment relationship who receives a fellowship-type stipend, this section makes no judgment on whether that money counts as "remuneration derived from teaching, lecturing or research" (「由于教学、讲学或研究取得的报酬」) under Article 20. Even if Japan exempts the tax, you may have to pay on the China side under Item 2 (report your Japanese income to China). An exemption does not mean you owe nothing on either side. This treaty was later modified by a multilateral convention. This section read the original treaty text and did not read the consolidated text.

### 4. Buying foreign exchange and converting it to RMB are each capped at USD 50,000 per person per year; above that you need supporting documents
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Arrange large transactions a few months ahead.
- In plain words: China gives each person a quota equivalent to USD 50,000 a year for converting foreign exchange into RMB, and a separate quota of the same size for converting RMB into foreign exchange. Within the quota you do it at a bank with your identity card, and you can have a direct relative do it for you. Above the quota, you must bring supporting documents showing the transaction amount to the bank.
- Benefit: Article 2 of the Detailed Rules for the Implementation of the Measures for the Administration of Individual Foreign Exchange (个人外汇管理办法实施细则): 「对个人结汇和境内个人购汇实行年度总额管理。年度总额分别为每人每年等值5万美元。」 ("Foreign exchange settlement by individuals and foreign exchange purchase by domestic individuals are subject to annual aggregate management. The annual aggregate for each is the equivalent of USD 50,000 per person per year.") Within the annual aggregate, you do it at a bank with your own valid identity document. Above it, current account transactions follow Articles 10 to 12. Article 4: purchase and settlement of foreign exchange within the annual aggregate 「可以委托其直系亲属代为办理」 ("may be entrusted to a direct relative to handle on one's behalf"). Article 12: where a domestic individual's non-business purchase of foreign exchange under the current account exceeds the annual aggregate, it is handled at a bank with the person's own valid identity document and relevant supporting documents showing the transaction amount.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国家外汇管理局 (2007). 个人外汇管理办法实施细则（汇发〔2007〕1 号，第二、四、十、十二条）. <https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2007/0105/22509.html>
- Notes: On this State Administration of Foreign Exchange page, the title is marked "amended" (「已修改」). This section read the text currently on the page and did not check article by article which provisions were later changed. The consequences of borrowing other people's quotas to split up purchases were written in Section 5, Item 38 and Section 31, Item 15 of the original book. This section did not verify them again. When you send money from Japan to China, the bank on the Japan side must submit a report to the tax office. See Section 3, Item 12 (the bank's report on a large remittance).

### 5. When you carry foreign currency cash into or out of China, declare anything above the equivalent of USD 5,000; in principle you cannot take out more than USD 10,000
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Fill in one declaration form on entry.
- In plain words: If you bring foreign currency cash into China and it exceeds the equivalent of USD 5,000, you must declare it to Customs in writing. If what you carry out does not exceed the amount you declared on your last entry, you are cleared on the basis of that declaration record. In principle you cannot carry out foreign currency cash exceeding the equivalent of USD 10,000. Send large amounts by bank remittance.
- Benefit: Article 3 of the Interim Measures for the Administration of Carrying Foreign Currency Cash into and out of China (携带外币现钞出入境管理暂行办法): 「入境人员携带外币现钞入境，超过等值5000美元的应当向海关书面申报，当天多次往返及短期内多次往返者除外。」 ("A person entering China who carries foreign currency cash exceeding the equivalent of USD 5,000 shall declare it to Customs in writing, except for persons making multiple trips on the same day or multiple trips within a short period.") Article 4 provides that if the foreign currency cash carried on exit does not exceed the amount declared on the most recent entry, there is no need to apply for a Permit for Carrying Foreign Exchange out of China (《携带外汇出境许可证》), and Customs clears it on the basis of the declaration record from the most recent entry. Article 7 provides that a person leaving China may take foreign currency out by bank remittance or by carrying drafts, traveller's cheques, international credit cards and the like, 「但原则上不得携带超过等值10000美元外币现钞出境」 ("but in principle shall not carry foreign currency cash exceeding the equivalent of USD 10,000 out of China").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国家外汇管理局、海关总署 (2003). 携带外币现钞出入境管理暂行办法（汇发〔2003〕102 号，第三、四、五、七条）. 国务院公报 2004 年第 5 号 <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2004/content_63085.htm>
- Notes: A person leaving China with no entry declaration record must apply to a bank for a Carrying Permit (《携带证》) for amounts from USD 5,000 up to USD 10,000. This is Article 5, and this section does not go into the details. The limits on RMB cash are in other rules. This section did not verify them. This section also did not verify Japanese Customs' declaration requirements for carrying cash in and out of Japan. Read Japan Customs' current explanation before you fly.

### 6. If you take Japanese nationality, your Chinese nationality is lost automatically; getting permanent residence does not affect nationality
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. This is an irreversible decision, and it is worth thinking through before applying for naturalization.
- In plain words: China does not recognize dual nationality. A Chinese citizen settled abroad who voluntarily takes a foreign nationality automatically loses Chinese nationality, with no procedure and without being asked whether they want to. Permanent residence is a status of residence, not a nationality. With permanent residence you are still a Chinese citizen. If you later want to restore Chinese nationality you must apply, and once approved you cannot keep the foreign nationality.
- Benefit: Article 3 of the Nationality Law (国籍法): 「中华人民共和国不承认中国公民具有双重国籍。」 ("The People's Republic of China does not recognize dual nationality for any Chinese citizen.") Article 9: 「定居外国的中国公民，自愿加入或取得外国国籍的，即自动丧失中国国籍。」 ("Any Chinese citizen who has settled abroad and who has voluntarily been naturalized as or acquired a foreign nationality automatically loses Chinese nationality.") Article 13: a foreign national who once held Chinese nationality may apply for restoration of Chinese nationality if they have legitimate reasons; 「被批准恢复中国国籍的，不得再保留外国国籍」 ("those whose applications for restoration of Chinese nationality are approved shall not retain foreign nationality"). Article 14: except as provided in Article 9, acquisition, loss and restoration of Chinese nationality require going through the application procedure.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (1980). 中华人民共和国国籍法（第三、九、十三、十四条）. 国家移民管理局刊登 <https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html>
- Notes: This section did not verify against the original text how household registration, the identity card, social insurance and real property in China are each handled after nationality is lost. For the Japan-side conditions for permanent residence and naturalization, see Section 1, Item 10 (what permanent residence looks at). This book has not yet written the conditions for naturalization.

### 7. A child born in Japan has Chinese nationality, as long as the child did not get a foreign nationality at birth
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. After the child is born you need to go to the embassy or consulate for documents. This section did not verify the procedure.
- In plain words: If both parents or one parent is a Chinese citizen and the child is born abroad, the child has Chinese nationality. The exception: if both parents or one parent is a Chinese citizen and has settled abroad, and the child has a foreign nationality at birth, the child does not have Chinese nationality.
- Benefit: Article 5 of the Nationality Law: 「父母双方或一方为中国公民，本人出生在外国，具有中国国籍；但父母双方或一方为中国公民并定居在外国，本人出生时即具有外国国籍的，不具有中国国籍。」 ("Any person born abroad whose parents are both Chinese citizens, or one of whose parents is a Chinese citizen, has Chinese nationality. But a person whose parents are both Chinese citizens and have both settled abroad, or one of whose parents is a Chinese citizen and has settled abroad, and who has acquired foreign nationality at birth, does not have Chinese nationality.")
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (1980). 中华人民共和国国籍法（第五条）. 国家移民管理局刊登 <https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html>
- Notes: Whether the child has Japanese nationality at birth is decided by Japan's Nationality Act. This section did not verify that law. This section did not verify any interpretation by the competent authority of what "settled abroad" (「定居在外国」) in the provision means, or whether holding Japanese permanent residence counts. It draws no conclusion. If one parent is Japanese, the child may have Japanese nationality at birth, and then this exception has to be looked at. This is this section's conjecture. Ask the embassy or consulate before the birth.

### 8. China's pension insurance counts cumulative years: you need 15 years to receive it monthly, and the individual account cannot be withdrawn early
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Check your contribution record once when you return to China.
- In plain words: Under China's basic old-age insurance for employees (职工基本养老保险), you can receive a monthly pension only if you have paid for a cumulative 15 years by retirement age. If you are short, you can keep paying until you reach 15 years. A gap in the middle does not reset the count, and if you change cities the years carry on. The money in the individual account cannot be taken out early. Years paid in Japan cannot be counted toward these 15 years.
- Benefit: Article 16 of the Social Insurance Law (社会保险法): an individual in the basic old-age insurance who has cumulative contributions of fifteen years or more on reaching the statutory retirement age receives the basic pension monthly; one with cumulative contributions of less than fifteen years 「可以缴费至满十五年，按月领取基本养老金」 ("may pay contributions until reaching fifteen years, and receive the basic pension monthly"), or may transfer to the old-age insurance for urban and rural residents. Article 14: 「个人账户不得提前支取」 ("the individual account may not be withdrawn early"), and if the individual dies, the balance of the individual account can be inherited. Article 19: where an individual takes up employment across pooling regions, the basic old-age insurance relationship moves with the person, and 「缴费年限累计计算」 ("contribution years are counted cumulatively").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018 年修正). 社会保险法（第十四、十六、十九条）. 中国政府网 <https://www.gov.cn/guoqing/2021-10/29/content_5647616.htm>
- Notes: Whether a person who settles abroad or loses Chinese nationality can withdraw the individual account in one lump sum is in the supporting rules to the Social Insurance Law. This section did not obtain the original text and draws no conclusion. This section did not verify local practice on whether you can keep paying in China by yourself while you are in Japan. Years in the two countries are not totalized. See Section 3, Item 5 (China and Japan have an agreement, but the contribution years cannot be combined). So if you have paid on both sides, check Japan's 10 years and China's 15 years separately.

### 9. If you are abroad and cannot renew your Chinese driving licence on time, apply for a deferral during its validity or within one year after expiry; the maximum is three years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. You apply to the vehicle administration office that issued the licence.
- In plain words: If you cannot renew or have your Chinese driving licence examined on time because you are abroad, you can apply to the vehicle administration office of the place of issue for a deferral, during the licence's validity or within one year after it expires. The deferral is at most three years, and you cannot drive during the deferral. If you return to China with a Japanese driving licence and want to change it for a Chinese one, you must take the Subject 1 test.
- Benefit: Article 75 of the Provisions on the Application for and Use of Motor Vehicle Driving Licences (机动车驾驶证申领和使用规定): a motor vehicle driver who, because of military service, going abroad (leaving the mainland) or similar reasons, cannot complete licence renewal on expiry, examination, or submission of proof of physical condition within the prescribed time 「可以在驾驶证有效期内或者有效期届满一年内向机动车驾驶证核发地车辆管理所申请延期办理」 ("may, within the validity period of the driving licence or within one year after its expiry, apply to the vehicle administration office of the place that issued the licence for a deferral"). ### 10. Part-time wages of students from China can be exempt from income tax, but you must submit the form through your employer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Fill in one form, attach a certificate of enrolment, and give one copy to each employer.
- In plain words: For a Chinese student who is in Japan solely to study, money received for living and study is exempt from income tax in Japan. The National Tax Agency states it plainly: part-time wages at a level that supplements living costs and tuition are included. You must get the form to the tax office through your employer before you first receive wages. If you did not file and tax was withheld, you can apply for a refund.
- Benefit: A National Tax Agency Q&A case (質疑応答事例) says: for a student staying in Japan solely to receive education who is now a resident of China or was a resident of China before the stay, 「その生計、教育のために受け取る給付又は所得は、免税とされます(日中租税協定第21条)」 ("payments or income received for their maintenance or education are exempt from tax (Article 21 of the Japan-China tax treaty)"). It goes on: 「中国から来日した大学生の日本での生活費や学費に充てる程度のアルバイト代であれば、免税とされます。」 ("Part-time wages of a university student who came to Japan from China are exempt from tax if they are at a level that goes toward living costs and tuition in Japan.") Note 1 of the same case states that for the exemption to apply at the withholding stage, the 「租税条約に関する届出書」 ("Application Form for Income Tax Convention") must be submitted through the payer of the wages to the district director of the tax office competent for the payer. In the same case, part-time income of students from India, Vietnam and Malaysia is not exempt because it is not paid from abroad, which shows that the treaties differ by country. The National Tax Agency procedure page states: an international student must attach a certificate of enrolment issued by their school; the time for filing is by the day before first receiving remuneration after entry; and one form is prepared for each payer. If you did not file and tax was withheld, you can submit the form and the refund claim form (Form 11, 様式 11) through your employer and get it back.
