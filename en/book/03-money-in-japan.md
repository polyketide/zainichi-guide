# 3. Money in Japan: pension, health insurance and tax

This section counts money. It covers three things that are deducted every month: pension, health insurance and tax. They have nothing to do with nationality. If you live in Japan, you pay. This section answers what you get back for paying, what you can take with you when you leave Japan, and how the Japanese and Chinese sides connect.

The legal text is the current text of the National Pension Act (国民年金法), the Employees' Pension Insurance Act (厚生年金保険法), the Health Insurance Act (健康保険法), the National Health Insurance Act (国民健康保険法), the Income Tax Act (所得税法), the Local Tax Act (地方税法) and others. Amounts come from the Japan Pension Service (日本年金機構) website. Everything is as of 2026-10-05. Each amount is followed by the fiscal year it applies to.

Paying these three on time is also one of the "public obligations" (「公的义务」) that the permanent residence screening looks at. Paying late and making it up afterwards still counts against you in principle. See Section 1, Item 10 (what permanent residence looks at).

Two things are not written in this section, because the original text was not obtained: tax-exempt investment accounts (NISA, iDeCo), and the monthly ceiling on out-of-pocket medical costs.

Order: first the things nobody can avoid, then the things you only use when leaving Japan or returning to China.

### 1. If you live in Japan between ages 20 and 60, you must join the National Pension (国民年金); the contribution is 17,920 yen a month
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 17,920 yen a month in fiscal year Reiwa 8 (令和8年度). For company employees, the company deducts Employees' Pension Insurance (厚生年金保険) from your pay, and you do not pay this amount separately.
- In plain words: Anyone who has a domicile in Japan and is aged 20 or over and under 60 is an insured person of the National Pension. Nationality does not matter. If no company pays for you, you pay it yourself: 17,920 yen a month, due by the end of the following month. The head of your household and your spouse are jointly liable for this money.
- Benefit: Article 7, paragraph 1, item 1 of the National Pension Act lists 「日本国内に住所を有する二十歳以上六十歳未満の者」 ("a person aged 20 or over and under 60 who has a domicile in Japan") as a Category 1 insured person (第1号被保険者). The text has no nationality condition. Article 88, paragraph 1 provides that an insured person must pay contributions. Paragraph 2 provides that the head of a household 「連帯して納付する義務を負う」 ("bears a joint and several obligation to pay") the contributions of insured persons in the same household. Paragraph 3 provides that spouses are jointly liable in the same way. The Japan Pension Service states that the National Pension contribution is 「1カ月あたり17,920円です（令和8年度）」 ("17,920 yen per month (fiscal year Reiwa 8)"), and that the payment deadline is 「納付対象月の翌月末日」 ("the last day of the month following the month the contribution is for").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国民年金法（昭和三十四年法律第百四十一号）第七条第一項、第八十八条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/334AC0000000141>；日本年金機構. 国民年金保険料. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/service/kokunen/hokenryo/hokenryo.html>
- Notes: The amount changes every fiscal year. Fiscal year Reiwa 8 runs from April 2026 to March 2027. Paying a period in advance in one payment gives a discount. This section did not verify how much the discount is. Employees' Pension Insurance for company employees is deducted as a proportion of pay. This section did not verify the rate. Researchers with no employment relationship, freelancers, and people who have left a job and not yet started the next one all belong to the group that must pay by themselves.

### 2. Students and people who cannot afford to pay: apply for the special system or an exemption instead of simply leaving it unpaid; you can pay back within 10 years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. You apply once every fiscal year.
- In plain words: A student whose income is below the standard can apply for the Special Payment System for Students (学生納付特例). During that time you do not have to pay contributions. That time counts toward the number of years needed to receive a pension, but it does not increase the amount of the pension. You can pay back within 10 years. If you applied, you are "not required to pay". If you did not apply, you are "in arrears".
- Benefit: Article 90-3, paragraph 1 of the National Pension Act provides that when an insured person who is a student or the like applies, and the previous year's income is at or below the amount set by Cabinet Order, the contributions for the designated period 「納付することを要しないものとし」 ("shall not be required to be paid"). Article 94, paragraph 1 provides that contributions that were exempted or covered by the student special system may be paid retroactively (追納) with the approval of the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare, limited to the part for 「十年以内の期間」 ("periods within ten years") before the month of approval. The Japan Pension Service page on the student special system states that this period is counted in the qualifying period (受給資格期間) for the Old-age Basic Pension (老齢基礎年金), and that retroactive payment from the third fiscal year onward carries an additional amount. The page's own words on retroactive payment are 「保険料が高くなることはあっても、安くなることはありません」 ("the contribution may become higher, but it never becomes lower").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国民年金法 第九十条の三、第九十四条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/334AC0000000141>；日本年金機構. 国民年金保険料の学生納付特例制度. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/service/kokunen/menjo/20150514.html>
- Notes: The specific amount of the income standard is in a Cabinet Order. This section did not verify it. Exemption and deferment for non-students are in Article 90 and several other articles. This section does not go into them. The wording of the permanent residence guidelines is that public obligations are fulfilled on time. The guidelines do not say how an approved exemption is assessed in the screening. This section draws no conclusion. It can only say that an approved exemption is not arrears.

### 3. To receive a pension in Japan, your years of contributions plus exemptions must add up to 10 years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Know where this line is.
- In plain words: The Old-age Basic Pension starts at age 65. If your contribution periods plus your exempted periods come to less than 10 years, you receive nothing at all. Your contribution years in China cannot be used to make up these 10 years in Japan.
- Benefit: Article 26 of the National Pension Act provides that the Old-age Basic Pension is paid to a person who has contribution-paid periods (保険料納付済期間) or contribution-exempted periods (保険料免除期間) 「六十五歳に達したとき」 ("when the person reaches age 65"). The proviso to the same article provides that if these two kinds of periods added together 「十年に満たないときは、この限りでない」 ("are less than ten years, this does not apply"). In the Japan Pension Service table of agreements, the entry for China reads 「加入期間の通算ができない」 ("periods of coverage cannot be totalized").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国民年金法 第二十六条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/334AC0000000141>；日本年金機構. 協定を結んでいる国との協定発効時期および対象となる社会保障制度. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/service/shaho-kyotei/kunibetsu/kyoteitimesystem.html>；日本年金機構. 脱退一時金の制度. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/service/jukyu/seido/sonota-kyufu/dattai-ichiji/20150406.html>
- Notes: Periods of Employees' Pension Insurance coverage also count toward these 10 years. This is the wording of the Japan Pension Service page on the lump-sum withdrawal payment (「厚生年金保険加入期間等を合算して10年間」, "10 years in total, adding together periods of Employees' Pension Insurance coverage and the like"). This section did not verify how you receive the pension abroad if you leave Japan after completing 10 years. Completing 10 years and taking the lump-sum withdrawal payment are an either-or choice. See Item 4 (claim the lump-sum withdrawal payment after leaving Japan).

### 4. If you leave Japan with less than 10 years, claim the lump-sum withdrawal payment (脱退一時金) within 2 years; it counts at most 5 years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. You mail the documents after leaving Japan, and you have to remember the 2-year deadline yourself.
- In plain words: If you do not have Japanese nationality, have paid into the pension for 6 months or more, and have not reached 10 years, you can claim part of the money back within 2 years after leaving Japan. The calculation counts at most 60 months. However long you paid, it counts only 5 years. For someone who paid the National Pension for a full 60 months, the amount in fiscal year Reiwa 8 is 537,600 yen. After you receive it, those years are reset to zero.
- Benefit: The conditions the Japan Pension Service lists for the National Pension lump-sum withdrawal payment are: you do not have Japanese nationality; you are not an insured person of a public pension system; your contribution-paid periods and the like total 6 months or more; you have not met the qualifying period for an old-age pension; you have never acquired the right to receive a Disability Basic Pension (障害基礎年金) or the like; you have no domicile in Japan; and no more than 2 years have passed since the day you last lost insured status, or, if you had a domicile in Japan when you lost that status, since the first day afterwards on which you had no domicile in Japan. The formula for the amount is the contribution amount of the fiscal year that contains your last contribution month, multiplied by one half, and then multiplied by a number set according to the number of months. Since April 2021 the cap on the number of months is 60 months. If your last contribution month falls between April 2026 and March 2027, the amount is 53,760 yen for 6 months or more and less than 12 months, 107,520 yen for 12 months or more and less than 18 months, 322,560 yen for 36 months or more and less than 42 months, and 537,600 yen for 60 months or more. The lump-sum withdrawal payment for Employees' Pension Insurance is calculated separately, as the average standard remuneration amount multiplied by a payment rate, with the same cap of 60 months. Article 9-3-2, paragraph 4 of the Supplementary Provisions of the National Pension Act and Article 29, paragraph 5 of the Supplementary Provisions of the Employees' Pension Insurance Act both provide that once the lump-sum withdrawal payment is received, for the insured period used as the basis of calculation the person 「被保険者でなかつたものとみなす」 ("shall be deemed not to have been an insured person").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 日本年金機構. 脱退一時金の制度（更新日 2026年4月1日）. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/service/jukyu/seido/sonota-kyufu/dattai-ichiji/20150406.html>；国民年金法 附則第九条の三の二. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/334AC0000000141>；厚生年金保険法（昭和二十九年法律第百十五号）附則第二十九条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/329AC0000000115>
- Notes: The formulas for both lump-sum withdrawal payments multiply by one half. What you get back is not everything you paid in. The next sentence is this section's inference, not the original text: for someone who has paid in Japan for more than 5 years, the months beyond 5 years do not go into this lump sum, and because periods are not totalized between China and Japan, it is worth working out "leave, or stay until 10 years" once around the time you complete 5 years. This section did not verify against the original text how much tax is withheld from the Employees' Pension Insurance lump-sum withdrawal payment, or whether you can then claim a tax refund. The pension system has been amended in recent years. This section did not check whether there are changes to the conditions for the lump-sum withdrawal payment that have been promulgated but are not yet in force.

### 5. China and Japan have a social security agreement, but the contribution years in the two countries cannot be combined
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain words: The China-Japan social security agreement took effect in September 2019. It covers Japan's public pensions and China's basic old-age insurance for employees (职工基本养老保险). It cannot add the contribution years in the two countries together.
- Benefit: In the Japan Pension Service table of agreements, the entry for China reads: the agreement took effect in 「2019年9月」 ("September 2019"); 「加入期間の通算ができない」 ("periods of coverage cannot be totalized"); the social security systems covered are 「公的年金制度」 ("public pension systems") for Japan and 「公的年金制度（被用者基本老齢保険）」 ("public pension system (basic old-age insurance for employees)") for China. In the same table, Finland and other countries are marked 「加入期間の通算ができる」 ("periods of coverage can be totalized").
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 日本年金機構. 協定を結んでいる国との協定発効時期および対象となる社会保障制度. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/service/shaho-kyotei/kunibetsu/kyoteitimesystem.html>
- Notes: The other function of the agreement is that people posted by their company to the other country do not have to pay on both sides. This section did not obtain the original text for the conditions and time limits of that part, so it is not written here. A person who finds a job in Japan on their own is not "posted" (「派遣」). Japan's health insurance and employment insurance are not in this agreement.

### 6. If illness keeps you from working, health insurance pays two-thirds of your pay from the 4th day, for up to 1 year and 6 months
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. A doctor and your company each have to fill in one section of certification.
- In plain words: If you are in a company health insurance plan and cannot work because of illness or injury, you get the Injury and Sickness Allowance (傷病手当金) from the 4th day you cannot work. The amount is two-thirds of the average daily standard remuneration over the previous 12 months. For the same illness you can receive it for up to 1 year and 6 months in total. National Health Insurance does not necessarily have this benefit.
- Benefit: Article 99, paragraph 1 of the Health Insurance Act provides that when an insured person cannot work because of medical treatment, the Injury and Sickness Allowance is paid 「三日を経過した日から」 ("from the day on which three days have passed") counting from the day the person became unable to work. Paragraph 2 provides that the amount per day is 「三分の二」 ("two-thirds") of one-thirtieth of the average of the standard monthly remuneration (標準報酬月額) for each of the 12 consecutive months up to the month payment begins. Paragraph 4 provides that the payment period for the same illness or injury is 「通算して一年六月間」 ("one year and six months in total") from the day payment begins. Voluntarily and continuously insured persons (任意継続被保険者) are not covered by paragraph 1. Article 58, paragraph 2 of the National Health Insurance Act provides that municipalities and associations 「条例又は規約の定めるところにより、傷病手当金の支給その他の保険給付を行うことができる」 ("may, as provided by ordinance or bylaws, pay the Injury and Sickness Allowance and provide other insurance benefits"). It says may, not must.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 健康保険法（大正十一年法律第七十号）第九十九条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/211AC0000000070>；国民健康保険法（昭和三十三年法律第百九十二号）第五十八条第二項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/333AC0000000192>
- Notes: For people with less than 12 months of coverage, paragraph 2 has a separate calculation. This section did not read how the amount is adjusted if the company still pays wages during the period. If you pay National Health Insurance yourself, ask the municipal office where you live whether it has this benefit.

### 7. When medical costs are very high, health insurance pays back the part above the ceiling
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Ask your insurer once before a hospital stay or major surgery.
- In plain words: Both company health insurance and National Health Insurance have the high-cost medical expense benefit (高額療養費). When the medical costs you pay yourself are clearly too high, the insurance pays the part above the ceiling. The ceiling amount is set in bands by income, in a Cabinet Order.
- Benefit: Article 115, paragraph 1 of the Health Insurance Act provides that the high-cost medical expense benefit is paid when the amount of co-payments and the like for medical treatment benefits 「著しく高額であるとき」 ("is extremely high"). Paragraph 2 provides that the conditions for payment and the amount paid are set by Cabinet Order, taking into account the effect of the burden of treatment costs on the household budget, and in particular on the household budget of people receiving long-term continuing treatment. Article 57-2 of the National Health Insurance Act has the same provision.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 健康保険法 第百十五条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/211AC0000000070>；国民健康保険法 第五十七条の二. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/333AC0000000192>
- Notes: How many yen the monthly ceiling is, and how to do the paperwork in advance so that the counter charges you only up to the ceiling, are in the Cabinet Order and the insurers' explanations. This section did not verify them, so it gives no amounts. Adjusting this ceiling has been under discussion in recent years. Before using it, read the current explanation from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare or from your own insurer. Meal costs and the like are not counted. The text says 「食事療養及び生活療養を除く」 ("excluding dietary treatment and living-support treatment").

### 8. After 5 years of living in Japan, Japan also taxes your foreign income; before that, foreign income is taxed only if it is paid in Japan or remitted to Japan
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Count how long you have lived in Japan over the past 10 years.
- In plain words: A foreign national with a domicile in Japan is a "resident" (「居住者」). If the time you have lived in Japan over the past 10 years totals no more than 5 years, you are a "non-permanent resident" (「非永住者」), and of your foreign income only the part paid in Japan and the part remitted into Japan is taxed. Once you pass 5 years, Japan taxes your income from the whole world.
- Benefit: Article 2, paragraph 1, item 3 of the Income Tax Act defines a resident (居住者) as an individual who has a domicile in Japan, or who has had a residence in Japan continuously for one year or more up to the present. Item 4 defines a non-permanent resident (非永住者) as, among residents, 「日本の国籍を有しておらず、かつ、過去十年以内において国内に住所又は居所を有していた期間の合計が五年以下である個人」 ("an individual who does not have Japanese nationality and whose periods of having a domicile or residence in Japan within the past ten years total five years or less"). Item 5 provides that an individual other than a resident is a non-resident (非居住者). Article 7, paragraph 1 sets the scope of taxation: for residents other than non-permanent residents, 「全ての所得」 ("all income"); for non-permanent residents, income other than foreign-source income, plus the foreign-source income 「国内において支払われ、又は国外から送金されたもの」 ("that is paid in Japan or remitted from abroad"); for non-residents, domestic-source income.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 所得税法（昭和四十年法律第三十三号）第二条第一項第三号から第五号まで、第七条第一項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/340AC0000000033>
- Notes: The 5 years is the total within the past 10 years. If you left Japan in between, add up the separate stretches. If you have rent, interest, dividends or other income in China, once you pass the 5-year line you must report it in Japan. If China has also taxed the same income, how to offset it depends on the foreign tax credit (外国税額控除) and the China-Japan tax treaty. This section did not read them. How the Chinese side decides whether you are a tax resident is a matter of Chinese tax law. It is placed in the section "Obligations in both countries".

### 9. If you have one salary and no more than 200,000 yen of other income, you do not have to file a tax return yourself; overpaid tax can be claimed back within 5 years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. If you do file, it is once a year and takes a few hours. This section did not verify the start and end dates for filing.
- In plain words: If your salary is 20 million yen or less, you are paid by only one employer and the year-end adjustment (年末調整) has been done, and your income other than salary totals no more than 200,000 yen, you do not have to file a final tax return (確定申告). If you overpaid tax and want it back, you can claim it at any time within 5 years.
- Benefit: Article 121, paragraph 1 of the Income Tax Act provides that a resident with employment income does not need to submit a final return if the amount of salary and the like is 「二千万円以下」 ("20 million yen or less"), the salary is received from only one payer, all of it has been subject to withholding or year-end adjustment, and the amount of income other than employment income and retirement income is 「二十万円以下」 ("200,000 yen or less"). Receiving salary from two or more payers falls under item 2 of the same paragraph, which has separate conditions. Article 74, paragraph 1 of the Act on General Rules for National Taxes (国税通則法) provides that a claim against the State for a refund or the like 「五年間行使しないことによつて、時効により消滅する」 ("is extinguished by prescription if it is not exercised for five years") from the day it can be claimed.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 所得税法 第百二十一条第一項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/340AC0000000033>；国税通則法（昭和三十七年法律第六十六号）第七十四条第一項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/337AC0000000066>
- Notes: Not having to file for income tax does not mean not having to file for resident tax (住民税). This section did not verify the rules on the resident tax side. People who left a job mid-year without a year-end adjustment, people who want to use deductions such as medical expenses, and people who want to claim parents in China as dependents get a refund only if they file. For a researcher with no employment relationship, which category of income the money falls under depends on the handbook of that program.

### 10. You can claim the deduction for dependents (扶養控除) for parents you support in China; for relatives aged 30 to 69 you must remit 380,000 yen or more a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Keep the proof of family relationship and the remittance records.
- In plain words: A relative living abroad can also count as a dependent relative, provided they share a livelihood with you and their total income for the year is 580,000 yen or less. Those aged 16 to 29 and those aged 70 or over count directly. Those aged 30 to 69 must be a person who went abroad to study, a person with a disability, or someone to whom you gave 380,000 yen or more in living or education expenses that year. The deduction is 380,000 yen per person, and 480,000 yen for those aged 70 or over.
- Benefit: Article 2, paragraph 1, item 34 of the Income Tax Act defines a dependent relative (扶養親族) as, among a resident's relatives (excluding the spouse) and the like, 「生計を一にするもの」 ("those who share a livelihood") with that resident and are 「合計所得金額が五十八万円以下である者」 ("persons whose total income amount is 580,000 yen or less"). Item 34-2 defines a dependent relative eligible for the deduction (控除対象扶養親族): for a resident, a person aged 16 or over; for a non-resident, 「年齢十六歳以上三十歳未満の者及び年齢七十歳以上の者」 ("persons aged 16 or over and under 30, and persons aged 70 or over"), and, among those aged 30 or over and under 70, a person who is one of the following: a person who has no domicile or residence in Japan because of study abroad; a person with a disability; a person who received from that resident in that year payments for living or education expenses of 「三十八万円以上」 ("380,000 yen or more"). Article 84, paragraph 1 provides that the deduction is 380,000 yen for each dependent relative eligible for the deduction, 630,000 yen for a specified dependent relative (特定扶養親族, aged 19 or over and under 23), and 480,000 yen for an elderly dependent relative (老人扶養親族, aged 70 or over).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 所得税法 第二条第一項第三十四号・第三十四号の二から第三十四号の四まで、第八十四条第一項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/340AC0000000033>
- Notes: The deduction is from income, not from the tax amount. What you actually save is the deduction multiplied by your tax rate. Which supporting documents you must submit (family relationship documents, remittance documents, with a translation attached if in a foreign language) is in other provisions and in the National Tax Agency's explanations. This section did not verify them. If your parents have a retirement pension, first confirm whether their total income is 580,000 yen or less. This section did not verify how a Chinese retirement pension is counted as income in Japan. It is best to remit to each person separately and keep the bank records. Cash carried back in person leaves no proof.

### 11. Wherever you live on January 1 gets that year's resident tax; work this out before leaving Japan at the start of a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain words: Resident tax (住民税) is decided by where you have a domicile on the single day of January 1. If your resident record (住民票) is in Japan on January 1, you owe the resident tax for that fiscal year, even if you leave Japan afterwards.
- Benefit: Article 39 of the Local Tax Act provides that the assessment date (賦課期日) for the individual prefectural inhabitant tax is 「当該年度の初日の属する年の一月一日」 ("January 1 of the year that contains the first day of that fiscal year"). Article 24, paragraph 1, item 1 lists an individual with a domicile in the prefecture as a person liable for the tax. Article 294, paragraph 1, item 1 lists an individual with a domicile in the municipality as a person liable for the municipal inhabitant tax, and paragraph 2 provides that this means a person recorded in the Basic Resident Register of that municipality.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 地方税法（昭和二十五年法律第二百二十六号）第二十四条第一項、第三十九条、第二百九十四条第一項・第二項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/325AC0000000226>
- Notes: The usual account is that resident tax is calculated on the previous year's income, so you still receive a bill in the year after you leave a job or return to China. ### 12. When you send a large amount of money to China, the bank reports that remittance to the tax office
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. State the purpose truthfully when you remit, and keep records of where the money came from.
- In plain words: When you send money abroad through a bank or receive money sent from abroad, and the amount exceeds the figure set by Cabinet Order, the bank must report that transaction to the tax office by the end of the following month: name, address, amount and reason for the remittance. This is not a penalty. It lets the tax office see it.
- Benefit: Article 4, paragraph 1 of the 内国税の適正な課税の確保を図るための国外送金等に係る調書の提出等に関する法律 (Act on Submission of Reports on Overseas Remittances etc. for the Purpose of Securing Proper Domestic Taxation) provides that when a customer of a financial institution makes an overseas remittance or the like through its business office, the financial institution must, except where the amount is at or below the amount set by Cabinet Order, prepare a 「国外送金等調書」 ("report on overseas remittances etc.") for each transaction and submit it to the competent district director of the tax office 「翌月末日までに」 ("by the last day of the month following") the month of the exchange transaction. For an overseas remittance, the items to be recorded are the customer's name, address, the remittance amount, the reason for the remittance stated on the notification form, and so on.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 内国税の適正な課税の確保を図るための国外送金等に係る調書の提出等に関する法律（平成九年法律第百十号）第四条第一項. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/409AC0000000110>
- Notes: The threshold amount is in the Enforcement Order. This section did not obtain it and gives no figure. Read this item together with Item 8 (after 5 years of living in Japan, foreign income is also taxed) and Item 10 (deduction for dependents for parents you support in China): remittance records are both the proof for the deduction for dependents and something the tax office can see.
