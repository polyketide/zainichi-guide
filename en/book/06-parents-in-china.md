# 6. Parents in China

This section counts money and time. When your parents are in China and you are in Japan, the hardest part is not being there: signing, paperwork and getting back all run half a step behind. This section covers only the few things that are added by being in Japan.

Guardianship for the elderly, wills, fraud prevention, long-term care insurance, how to save on medical costs, how to keep medical records, and the procedures after a death are already covered in detail in Sections 17, 24 and 25 of the original book, 高性价比人生指南 (HowToLiveBetter). They are written according to the rules of mainland China and apply directly when your parents are in China, so they are not repeated here. For the limit on sending money to your parents, see Section 4, Item 4 (buying and selling foreign exchange, 50,000 US dollars a year). For claiming the dependent deduction in Japan, see Section 3, Item 10 (dependent deduction for supporting parents in China).

The sources are the original text of China's Civil Code (民法典), the service notices of the Chinese Embassy in Japan, Japan's Inheritance Tax Act (相続税法) and the website of the Immigration Services Agency (入管庁), as of 2026-10-05.

### 1. To have family in China handle things for you, go in person to the embassy or consulate and have a power of attorney notarized
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: The notarization fee follows the embassy's fee table; this section did not verify the amount. You book an appointment and go to the embassy or consulate. The certificate is usually collected on the 4th working day.
- In plain words: For matters in China such as unlocking a bank card, transferring a household registration or a lawsuit, you can write a power of attorney so that family can act for you, but the other side usually wants it notarized. In Japan you can do this at a Chinese embassy or consulate. Before you go, complete the pre-review and book an appointment in the "中国领事" (China Consular Affairs) app. Do not sign the power of attorney in advance.
- Benefit: Article 165 of the Civil Code (民法典): a written power of attorney shall state the agent's name, the matters entrusted, the scope and the period of authority, and shall be signed or sealed by the principal. The notarization guide of the Chinese Embassy in Japan lists several kinds of power of attorney notarization: a general power of attorney notarization is 「用于委托他人给孩子上户口、更新护照，办理银行卡激活、解锁，户口迁移等事宜」 ("used to entrust another person with matters such as registering a child's household registration, renewing a passport, activating or unlocking a bank card, and transferring a household registration"); a litigation power of attorney notarization is used to entrust another person to litigate for you in a court in China. The guide states: you must pass the pre-review in the "中国领事" app and then book an appointment to come to the embassy; for declarations and powers of attorney, 「请勿事先签名或填写日期」 ("please do not sign or fill in the date in advance"); once approved, the certificate is usually collected on the 4th working day from the date of acceptance, not counting time for supplementary materials or for checks with China; the applicant may prepare the text of the power of attorney according to the requirements of the receiving institution, and the embassy's template is for reference only; notarial certificates not collected within half a year are voided and destroyed.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一百六十五条）. 最高人民法院刊登 <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html>；中华人民共和国驻日本国大使馆. 公证申办须知. <https://jp.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_0/zjfw_138408/zhjfw2/202405/t20240507_11300531.htm>
- Notes: In search results, the snippets of embassy and consulate pages say that the applicant must come in person, may not send someone else, and must bring a passport and residence card. These two points were not found as original sentences in the embassy page that was retrieved. This section did not verify them; read the original guide page before you go. Before applying, ask the receiving institution in China what format it wants and whether it accepts the embassy's notarization. For matters such as property sales, requirements differ from place to place. People living in Osaka, Fukuoka and other areas come under the local consulate general; this section did not check each consulate's guide one by one.

### 2. For a document issued in Japan to be used in China, a Japanese apostille is enough; the embassy and consulates no longer do legalization
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: You apply to the competent Japanese authority. This section did not verify the fee or the number of days.
- In plain words: Since 7 November 2023, a public document issued in Japan needs only a Japanese apostille (附加证明书) to be used in mainland China. It no longer goes through legalization by the Chinese embassy or consulates, and the embassy has stopped that service. Having the apostille does not guarantee that the institution in China will accept the document. Ask them first what they need.
- Benefit: The notice of the Chinese Embassy in Japan states: 「11月7日起，日本签发的《公约》范围内的公文书只需办理日本附加证明书 (Apostille) 即可送往中国内地使用，无需办理日本和中国驻日本使领馆的领事认证。」 ("From 7 November, public documents issued in Japan that fall within the scope of the Convention need only a Japanese apostille to be sent for use in mainland China, and no longer need consular legalization by Japan and by the Chinese embassy or consulates in Japan.") From the same day the embassy stopped its consular legalization service, and for documents issued in Japan and intended for use in mainland China you apply to the competent Japanese authority for an apostille. The notice also states that an apostille certifies the authenticity of the signature on the public document, the capacity in which the signer acted, and where necessary that the seal is genuine, and that 「办妥日本附加证明书不代表该公文书必然被中国用文单位接受」 ("obtaining a Japanese apostille does not mean that the public document will necessarily be accepted by the receiving institution in China"). It advises asking the receiving institution in China beforehand about its requirements on format, content, time limits, translation and so on.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国驻日本国大使馆 (2023). 关于中国加入《取消外国公文书认证要求的公约》后我馆停办领事认证业务的通知. <https://jp.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_0/zjfw_138408/zhjfw2/202310/t20231024_11167059.htm>
- Notes: Which documents count as "public documents" (公文书), and whether a privately written document must first go through a Japanese notary, is in the explanation by Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The embassy notice gives the address of that page; this section did not retrieve it. How to handle each of the common documents, such as a resident record (住民票), a tax payment certificate or a certificate of employment, was not verified in this section. In the other direction, a document issued in China for use in Japan goes through the apostille on the Chinese side, which this section does not cover.

### 3. If a parent needs surgery and you cannot get back in time: the doctor must explain to the patient and get the patient's consent, and close relatives come in only when the patient cannot
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Agree in advance with your parents and relatives in China on who will sign, and leave them your contact details.
- In plain words: For surgery, special examinations and special treatment, the doctor must first explain the risks and the alternatives to the patient and get the patient's explicit consent. Only when the patient cannot be told or should not be told does the doctor explain to close relatives and get their consent. When a patient's life is in danger and neither the patient nor close relatives can be reached, treatment can start at once with the approval of the person in charge of the hospital. It does not wait for you.
- Benefit: Article 1219 of the Civil Code: where surgery, a special examination or special treatment is needed, medical staff shall promptly explain to the patient in specific terms the medical risks, alternative treatment plans and other circumstances, and obtain the patient's explicit consent; 「不能或者不宜向患者说明的，应当向患者的近亲属说明，并取得其明确同意」 ("where it is impossible or inappropriate to explain to the patient, the explanation shall be given to the patient's close relatives, and their explicit consent shall be obtained"). Article 1220: 「因抢救生命垂危的患者等紧急情况，不能取得患者或者其近亲属意见的，经医疗机构负责人或者授权的负责人批准，可以立即实施相应的医疗措施。」 ("Where, in an emergency such as the rescue of a patient whose life is in danger, the opinion of the patient or the patient's close relatives cannot be obtained, the corresponding medical measures may be carried out immediately with the approval of the person in charge of the medical institution or an authorized person in charge.")
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一千二百一十九条、第一千二百二十条）. 最高人民法院刊登 <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html>
- Notes: The provision says "close relatives" (近亲属). It sets no order among them and does not require a child, so the parent's spouse or siblings in China can sign. Whether a hospital will in practice insist that a child be present, or accept consent by phone or video, is not in the provision; this section did not verify any health authority rules against the original text. The measure is set as mortality because this item bears on whether treatment is delayed. The size of the benefit is judged from the consequences; there are no figures in the provision.

### 4. On a trip home, go with your parents to set up voluntary guardianship; your living abroad does not stop the document from being valid
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: A notarization fee of a few hundred yuan (the original book's estimate). Your parent and the person designated must both be present; fit it into one trip home.
- In plain words: While your parents are of clear mind, they can decide in writing in advance who will be their guardian in the future. This is voluntary guardianship (意定监护). They can name a close relative, or another individual or organization willing to serve. If you live abroad year after year, it is worth discussing with your parents whether to name someone in China, or to write down clearly how duties are split between you and a relative in China.
- Benefit: Article 33 of the Civil Code: 「具有完全民事行为能力的成年人，可以与其近亲属、其他愿意担任监护人的个人或者组织事先协商，以书面形式确定自己的监护人，在自己丧失或者部分丧失民事行为能力时，由该监护人履行监护职责。」 ("An adult with full capacity for civil conduct may, through prior consultation with close relatives or with other individuals or organizations willing to serve as guardian, determine his or her own guardian in writing; when the adult loses or partly loses capacity for civil conduct, that guardian shall perform the duties of guardianship.")
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第三十三条）. 最高人民法院刊登 <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html>
- Notes: The provision does not require the guardian to live in China, and does not require notarization. Notarization and the fee of a few hundred yuan are what Section 17, Item 1 of the original book says; this section did not verify them again. How a guardian living abroad actually performs the duties, and whether hospitals and banks accept it, was not verified in this section. Whether two people can be named at once and how to split duties is not in the provision. For the form of a will, see Section 17, Item 2 of the original book.

### 5. Inheritance after your parents die: spouse, children and parents are first in order; if you do not want to inherit, renounce in writing before the estate is divided
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. If you are abroad, both renouncing the inheritance and entrusting someone else to handle it need the notarized power of attorney described in Item 1 (get a power of attorney notarized at the embassy or consulate).
- In plain words: Where there is no will, the spouse, children and parents are the heirs first in order. If you do not want to inherit, you must renounce in writing before the estate is disposed of; saying nothing counts as accepting. If you inherit, you pay the deceased's taxes and debts only up to the actual value of what you inherit. An heir who renounces may bear no liability for paying them.
- Benefit: Article 1127 of the Civil Code: the estate is inherited in order; first in order are the spouse, children and parents, and second in order are siblings, paternal grandparents and maternal grandparents; where there is an heir first in order, those second in order do not inherit. Article 1124: 「继承开始后，继承人放弃继承的，应当在遗产处理前，以书面形式作出放弃继承的表示；没有表示的，视为接受继承。」 ("After succession opens, an heir who renounces the inheritance shall express the renunciation in writing before the estate is disposed of; where no such expression is made, the heir is deemed to have accepted the inheritance.") Article 1161: an heir shall pay the taxes and debts that the deceased is legally required to pay, up to the actual value of the estate received; an heir who renounces the inheritance may bear no liability for paying these taxes and debts.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一千一百二十四、一千一百二十七、一千一百六十一条）. 最高人民法院刊登 <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html>
- Notes: What is actually needed to transfer property or withdraw deposits (inheritance notarization, a court judgment, banks' small-sum withdrawal procedures) was not verified in this section. For the procedures after a death, see Section 25 of the original book. Which country's law applies when an heir is abroad or has already taken foreign nationality is in the Law on the Application of Law to Foreign-Related Civil Relations (涉外民事关系法律适用法); this section did not obtain the original text. Whether China currently has an estate tax was not verified in this section and is not stated. For whether tax is due on the Japanese side, see Item 6 (Japan's inheritance tax).

### 6. If you inherit your parents' estate in China, Japan may also charge inheritance tax (相続税): permanent residents and people who have lived here more than 10 years should take care
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Count how many of the past 15 years you have lived in Japan, and check which category your status of residence belongs to.
- In plain words: For a person living in Japan at the time of the inheritance, in principle all the property inherited counts for Japan's inheritance tax, including property in China. The exception: you hold a status of residence such as work or study, the time you have lived in Japan in the past 15 years totals no more than 10 years, and the parent who died did not live in Japan. In that case only property in Japan is taxed. Permanent residents do not hold that kind of status. There is a tax-free amount: 30 million yen plus 6 million yen times the number of heirs. Above that, you must file within 10 months from the day after you learn of the death.
- Benefit: Article 1-3, paragraph 1, item 1 of the Inheritance Tax Act (相続税法) provides that a person who acquires property by inheritance or bequest and has an address in Japan at the time of acquisition is a taxpayer, in two categories: (イ) 「一時居住者でない個人」 ("an individual who is not a temporary resident"); (ロ) a temporary resident (一時居住者), except where the decedent is a foreign decedent (外国人被相続人) or a non-resident decedent (非居住被相続人). Article 2 provides that for a person falling under item 1 or item 2, 「財産の全部に対し、相続税を課する」 ("inheritance tax is imposed on all of the property") acquired. Article 1-3, paragraph 1, item 3 provides that a person who acquires property located in Japan, has an address in Japan at the time of acquisition and does not fall under item 1 is also a taxpayer, limited to the property in Japan. Paragraph 3, item 1 defines a temporary resident as a person who, when the inheritance opens, holds a status of residence under Appended Table I of the Immigration Control Act (入管法), and whose periods of having an address in Japan within the 15 years before the inheritance opens total 「十年以下」 ("ten years or less"). Item 3 defines a non-resident decedent as a decedent who has no address in Japan when the inheritance opens, including one who at no time within the 10 years before the inheritance opens had an address in Japan. Article 15 provides that the basic deduction (基礎控除) for the estate is 「三千万円と六百万円に当該被相続人の相続人の数を乗じて算出した金額との合計額」 ("the total of 30 million yen and the amount found by multiplying 6 million yen by the number of that decedent's heirs"). Article 27 provides that where the total taxable value exceeds the basic deduction and there is inheritance tax payable, a return must be filed 「十月以内」 ("within ten months") from the day after the day the person learned that the inheritance had opened.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 相続税法（昭和二十五年法律第七十三号）第一条の三、第二条、第十五条、第二十七条. e-Gov 法令検索 <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/325AC0000000073>
- Notes: Permanent Resident, Spouse or Child of Japanese National and Long-Term Resident are statuses under Appended Table II and are not within the definition of "temporary resident". This is a literal reading of the provision; this section did not verify the National Tax Agency's commentary. A person on a study or work status who passes the 10-year line likewise becomes taxable on all property. How property in China is valued in Japan, whether taxes and fees paid on the Chinese side can be credited, and how it works when your siblings are in China and only you are in Japan were all not verified in this section. The "number of heirs" in the basic deduction is counted under Japan's Civil Code; how to count when the parent is Chinese was not verified in this section. If the amount is large, have a tax accountant (税理士) who knows both countries look at it once while your parents are alive. Gifts made during life fall under a separate gift tax, whose taxpayer rules have a similar structure; this section did not read them.

### 7. Bringing your parents to live in Japan long term does not work on an ordinary work visa; highly skilled professionals can when a child is under 7 or a spouse is pregnant
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Several conditions must be met at the same time; check them through first.
- In plain words: The Immigration Services Agency states that a foreigner with a work-type status of residence cannot, in principle, bring parents to live in Japan. Highly skilled foreign professionals (高度外国人材) have an exception: to look after a child under 7 of the professional or the spouse, or to care for a pregnant spouse or the pregnant professional. The conditions are a household income of 8 million yen or more a year, living with you, and only the parents of either you or your spouse.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency's page on preferential treatment for highly skilled professionals says: 「現行制度では、就労を目的とする在留資格で在留する外国人の親の受入れは認められませんが」 ("Under the current system, admitting the parents of foreign nationals residing under a status of residence for the purpose of work is not permitted, but"), in the following cases the entry and residence of a parent (including an adoptive parent) of the highly skilled foreign professional or of the spouse is permitted under certain conditions: (1) raising a child under 7 (including an adopted child) of the highly skilled foreign professional or the spouse; (2) giving assistance and the like to the pregnant spouse of the highly skilled foreign professional or to the pregnant highly skilled foreign professional. The main conditions are: the household income of the highly skilled foreign professional (the professional's and the spouse's combined) is 8 million yen or more a year; living together with the highly skilled foreign professional; limited to the parents of either the highly skilled foreign professional or the spouse.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁. 高度人材ポイント制による出入国在留管理上の優遇措置. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/newimmiact_3_preferential_index.html>
- Notes: This opening exists for childcare and for caring for a pregnant woman, not for supporting parents in old age. What happens to the parent's status after the child turns 7 is not written in this part of the page; this section did not verify it. Whether parents who are old and have no one to care for them in China can come to live in Japan long term: the Immigration Services Agency's published notices have no such category, this section found no official page, and it is not stated here. Short family visits go through a Temporary Visitor visa (短期滞在); the conditions are in the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' explanation, which this section did not retrieve. Whether permanent residents can bring parents was also not verified.
