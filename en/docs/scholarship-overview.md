# Scholarships for international students: an overview

This page brings together the grant-type support that international students in Japan, and people who have just received a doctorate, can apply for: who pays, how much a month, who can apply, where to apply. It also lists a few schemes that international students cannot get, so that you do not waste a trip.

Only what could be read in official original texts is written here, as of 2026-10-05. Each amount is followed by the year given in the original. Scholarships change every year; check the current year's application guidelines before applying.

This page supplements Section 8, Item 8 of the main text (the Honors Scholarship). The Chinese version ([docs/奖学金一览.md](../../docs/奖学金一览.md)) is authoritative; where versions differ, the Chinese text prevails.

## Four things first

- Most scholarships are applied for after you have arrived in Japan and entered a school, through the school you are enrolled in. Study in Japan's wording: 「ほとんどの奨学金は、日本に来てから学校に入学した後に、日本国内で申し込むことになります」 ("most scholarships are applied for within Japan, after coming to Japan and entering a school").
- Few cover all costs. The same page says: 「奨学金には留学に必要な全費用を支給するものは少なく、多くは生活費や授業料の一部に充てるためのものです」 ("few scholarships pay the full cost of studying abroad; most are meant to cover part of living costs or tuition").
- What can be applied for before coming to Japan is mainly three things: the government (MEXT) scholarship (embassy or university recommendation), the Honors Scholarship reservation for high scorers in the EJU, and a small number of local and private scholarships with pre-arrival selection.
- People whose status of residence is 「留学」 (Student) cannot apply for the JASSO grant scholarship meant for Japanese students; see Part 6 below.

## 1. Japanese Government (MEXT) Scholarship, the "kokuhi" (国費) scholarship

There are 7 types. Tuition is not charged and a round-trip air ticket is provided. Ages and amounts are as follows, from the 『日本留学奨学金パンフレット 2026-2027』 (Scholarships for International Students in Japan, 2026-2027) and the Study in Japan pages.

| Type | Age | Monthly amount | Period | How to apply |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research Students (graduate level) | under 35 | non-degree students 143,000 yen; master's 144,000 yen; doctoral 145,000 yen | non-degree up to 2 years; degree students within the standard period of study | embassy recommendation; university recommendation |
| Teacher Training Students | under 35 | 143,000 yen | up to 1 year 6 months including Japanese-language training | embassy recommendation |
| Undergraduate Students | under 25 | 117,000 yen | by embassy recommendation, 5 years including Japanese-language training (7 years for medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, veterinary medicine) | embassy recommendation; university recommendation |
| Japanese Studies Students | 18 or older and under 30 | 117,000 yen | 1 year | embassy recommendation; university recommendation |
| College of Technology Students | under 25 | 117,000 yen | 4 years including Japanese-language training | embassy recommendation; college recommendation |
| Specialized Training College Students | under 25 | 117,000 yen | 3 years including Japanese-language training | embassy recommendation |
| Young Leaders' Program (YLP) | in principle under 40 | 242,000 yen | 1 year | recommendation by a public recommending body abroad |

- After the amounts for Research, Teacher Training and Undergraduate Students, the pamphlet notes 「地域加算あり」 ("regional supplement available"). How much, this page did not verify.
- Research Students must be qualified to enter a Japanese master's or doctoral program. Undergraduate Students must have completed 12 years of schooling. Japanese Studies Students must be enrolled at an undergraduate faculty outside Japan at the time of arrival and of return, majoring in Japanese language or culture.
- **Embassy recommendation**: apply to the Japanese embassy or consulate in your own country. The pamphlet says: 「募集対象となる種類は国・地域によって異なります」 ("which types are recruited differs by country and region"). Which types the Japanese missions in China recruit in a given year, and when, this page did not verify; see the mission's notice for that year. Candidates can state a preference for the school; the final decision is MEXT's.
- **University recommendation**: a Japanese university selects candidates under inter-university exchange agreements and the like, and recommends them to MEXT. Study in Japan states that not every university has a recommendation quota, that conditions and grade standards differ by university, and that anyone considering this route should first check whether the university they want has a quota.
- For self-financed students already in Japan, the Study in Japan table says: 「留学生として既に日本にいる人も、一定の条件のもと応募可能な推薦枠がある」 ("there is a recommendation quota that people already in Japan as international students can apply for under certain conditions"). Which quota and which conditions, this page could not verify against the original text; ask the international student office at your school.

## 2. The two JASSO scholarships

| Name | Who | Monthly amount | Period | How to apply |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 留学生受入れ促進プログラム（文部科学省外国人留学生学習奨励費） (MEXT Honors Scholarship for Privately-Financed International Students) | self-financed students with the status of residence 「留学」: graduate school, research students, undergraduate, junior college, college of technology (3rd year and above), specialized training college (post-secondary course), international student departments, preparatory courses | 48,000 yen | in principle 1 year from April to the following March, or 6 months from October to the following March | through the school you are enrolled in |
| same | students at Japanese-language institutions | 30,000 yen | same | through the school you are enrolled in |
| 海外留学支援制度（協定受入） (Student Exchange Support Program, inbound) | students who remain enrolled at a university abroad and are accepted in Japan for 8 days to 1 year under a student exchange agreement | 80,000 yen | 8 days to 1 year | through your current university |

Conditions for the Honors Scholarship (Study in Japan page):

- A grade point average coefficient of 2.30 or higher in the previous academic year, expected to be maintained.
- Japanese or English at the required level: JLPT N2 or above, or 200 points or more in the Japanese subject of the EJU (reading, listening and listening-reading), or English at CEFR B2 or above. Students in international student departments, preparatory courses and Japanese-language institutions are exempt from this condition.
- Remittances from home (excluding admission fees, tuition and the like) averaging 90,000 yen a month or less.
- If you have a supporter living in Japan, their annual income is under 5 million yen.
- You are not receiving a scholarship whose combination with the Honors Scholarship is restricted, and not receiving support under JASSO's Student Exchange Support Program and the like.
- Students at Japanese-language institutions must also intend to go on to a university or the like in the year after the grant.
- The status of residence must be 「留学」. The page states that people with other statuses such as 「家族滞在」 (Dependent) or 「定住者」 (Long-Term Resident) are not eligible.
- Each school can recommend a different number of people; the page says 「学校によっては申込みができないことがあります」 ("at some schools you may not be able to apply"). The pamphlet gives the number recruited in fiscal 2025 as 6,611.

**Reservation before coming to Japan**: self-financed students who score highly in the EJU and then newly enter an undergraduate faculty, junior college, college of technology (3rd year and above) or specialized training college (post-secondary course) as regular students can be selected as reserved recipients of the Honors Scholarship. You opt in when registering for the EJU, in the 「留学生受入れ促進プログラム予約制度」 field. Graduate schools are not among the destinations covered by this reservation system. How many are selected and from what score, this page did not verify.

The inbound exchange program has a condition that, if you receive other scholarships, their combined monthly amount must not exceed 80,000 yen.

## 3. Doctoral course: JSPS Research Fellowships for Young Scientists (DC)

Strictly speaking this is a research grant, not a scholarship, but it is one of the main sources of living costs for doctoral students, and foreign nationals can apply.

| Name | Who | Monthly amount | Period |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research Fellow DC1 | as of 1 April of the year of appointment, enrolled in a Japanese doctoral program at a level corresponding to the 1st year of the latter stage, and so on; 「外国人も含む」 ("including foreign nationals") | 200,000 to 230,000 yen (「支給予定額」, "planned amount") | 3 years |
| Research Fellow DC2 | enrolled at a level corresponding to the 2nd year or later of the latter stage of a doctoral program, and so on; 「外国人も含む」 | 200,000 to 230,000 yen (「支給予定額」) | 2 years |

- The JSPS page says 「研究奨励金の額は変更することがあります」 ("the amount of the research grant may change").
- Anyone who has previously been a Research Fellow (DC, PD, SPD) cannot apply.
- The application period, selection method and obligations after appointment (duty to devote yourself to research, whether other income may be combined) this page did not read; see the JSPS application guidelines for the year.
- How the status of residence is handled after appointment, this page did not verify.

## 4. SPRING (次世代研究者挑戦的研究プログラム): the living-cost part is for Japanese students only

This is support that JST provides to doctoral students through universities. The JST President's statement of 9 July 2025 says that MEXT has set out a policy for this program: 「研究奨励費（生活費相当額）支援の対象は日本人学生に限定する」 ("the support in the form of a research incentive (living-cost equivalent) is limited to Japanese students"). The same statement names the Japanese Government Scholarship as the representative scheme for supporting outstanding international students.

From which fiscal year this policy applies, how international students already receiving it are treated, and whether international students can still receive the research-cost part, the statement does not say, and this page could not verify against the original text. If you are in or considering a doctoral program, ask the SPRING office at your university.

## 5. After the doctorate: two JSPS fellowships whose nationality conditions are mirror images

| Name | Nationality condition | Who | Monthly amount | Period |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research Fellow PD | 「日本国籍を持つ者、又は日本に永住を許可されている外国人」 ("a person of Japanese nationality, or a foreign national granted permanent residence in Japan") | as of 1 April of the year of appointment, less than 5 years since receiving the doctorate (expected receipt is acceptable at application); the host institution must be a different institution from the one that awarded the degree (「研究機関移動」, "change of research institution"); another campus of the same university does not count | 362,000 yen (「支給予定額」) | 3 years |
| JSPS Postdoctoral Fellowship for Research in Japan (Standard), 外国人特別研究員（一般） | nationality of a country with which Japan has diplomatic relations; 「日本国籍を持つ者及び日本に永住を許可されている外国人は対象外」 ("persons of Japanese nationality and foreign nationals granted permanent residence in Japan are not eligible") | as of 1 April of the year of appointment, less than 6 years since receiving the doctorate, or expected to receive it by the start of the fellowship; whether or not you hold a full-time post does not matter; previous fellows (Standard, or Pathway to University Positions) are not eligible | maintenance allowance 362,000 yen; plus a settling-in allowance of 200,000 yen, a round-trip international air ticket and overseas travel insurance | 12 to 24 months |

- Reading the two pages literally: a Chinese national with a doctorate and no permanent residence can apply for the Postdoctoral Fellowship for Research in Japan but not for PD; once permanent residence is granted, the reverse.
- The Postdoctoral Fellowship (Standard) has two routes, 「公募」 (open application) and 「推薦」 (nomination). Under open application, the head of the Japanese host researcher's university or research institution applies, and one host researcher can submit up to 3 applications; under nomination, an overseas nominating authority nominates. So you need to find a host researcher first.
- People already living in Japan before the start date do not receive the outbound air ticket or the settling-in allowance. The host researcher can apply, through their institution, for the 「特別研究員奨励費」 (Grant-in-Aid for JSPS Fellows).
- In counting "less than 6 years" since the doctorate, periods of maternity leave and childcare leave are excluded.
- The PD page says 「研究奨励金の額は変更することがあります」. "Employed PD" fellows hired by the host institution are paid a salary by the institution.
- Not verified: the application periods and acceptance rates of the two; the status of residence and the taxation of the maintenance allowance for the Postdoctoral Fellowship; the conditions of the Pathway to University Positions variant; the terms of project researchers and postdoctoral researchers employed by universities themselves (each institution advertises its own posts; there is no single original text).

## 6. What international students cannot get: the JASSO grant scholarship (高等教育の修学支援新制度)

JASSO's eligibility page states that foreign nationals can apply only if their status of residence is one of the following four.

1. Special permanent residents under the statute.
2. 「永住者」 (Permanent Resident), 「日本人の配偶者等」 (Spouse or Child of Japanese National) or 「永住者の配偶者等」 (Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident).
3. 「定住者」 (Long-Term Resident) who intends to live permanently in Japan.
4. 「家族滞在」 (Dependent) who meets the conditions in the page's notes (graduation from Japanese elementary, junior high and senior high schools, and so on).

「留学」 (Student) is not among the four. The conditions of JASSO's loan scholarships for foreign nationals, this page did not read.

## 7. Local governments, international exchange associations and private foundations

JASSO publishes the 『日本留学奨学金パンフレット』 every year, listing these scholarships in tables. The 2026-2027 edition's data are as of February 2026 and cover only bodies that submitted entries. Counting by the table numbers, there are 20-odd local government and international exchange association scholarships and 80-odd private ones, plus some with pre-arrival selection. This page does not transcribe them one by one: the pamphlet is reissued every year, and a transcription would be out of date the next year. Only how to read it is given here.

How to read the pamphlet's tables:

- If the 「質問」 (enquiries) column says 「学」, ask your school's office; if it says 「団」, ask the scholarship body directly. The 「応募」 (application) column likewise distinguishes applying through the school from applying to the body directly.
- The 「支給対象」 (eligible students) column uses abbreviations: 専 (specialized training college, post-secondary course), 短 (junior college), 学 (undergraduate), 研 (graduate-level research student), 修 (master's), 博 (doctoral), 日 (Japanese-language institution), 別 (international student department).
- Where the 「出身国・地域」 (country or region of origin) column has a restriction, only students from those countries and regions are eligible.
- The 「重複受給」 (concurrent receipt) column shows whether other scholarships may be held at the same time, and the limit.
- The 「「留学」以外」 column shows whether people whose status of residence is not 「留学」 can apply.
- Amounts are in thousands of yen: 「80/月」 means 80,000 yen a month.

Most local scholarships require you to live in the area or be enrolled at a school in the area.

Private foundations differ widely in amount, target countries and designated schools. Go through the pamphlet and filter by your own school, course and nationality. The pamphlet does not list grants for researchers; it points to the websites of the Japan Society for the Promotion of Science and the Japan Foundation Center.

## 8. Schools' own scholarships and tuition reductions

Study in Japan states that many schools have their own scholarships and tuition reductions, some of which can be applied for before admission. The pamphlet does not cover this category. Two ways to look: Study in Japan's 「奨学金・授業料減免制度検索」 (scholarship and tuition reduction search), and the website of the school you want. The conditions at individual schools, this page did not verify.

## 9. The Chinese government side

For the China Scholarship Council's state-sponsored graduate program and the Chinese Government Award for Outstanding Self-Financed Students Abroad, this page **could not verify the original text**: the CSC website (csc.edu.cn) could not be fetched by program, and opening it in a browser returned no content; no matching section was found on the home page of the Chinese Embassy in Japan. So no amounts, conditions or dates are given here. Look at the CSC website's selection notice for the year directly, or ask your school or the embassy's education section.

People receiving a Chinese government scholarship count as "students dispatched by a foreign government" and are outside the scope of the Honors Scholarship: the JASSO page states that self-financed international students exclude government-sponsored students and students dispatched by foreign governments. See Section 8, Item 8 of the main text.
